Johnson in First Feature
Win at O'Hare Stadium
CHICAGO, JULY 3-Lanky
Eric Johnson of Highland Park,
Ill., a veteran of all types of
midwestern stock car race ac-
tion, tonight posted his first
main event victory in NASCAR
late model competition here at
O'Hare Stadium and set a new
track record for 35 laps in the
process.
Driving the Martinelli Bros.,
'57 Chevrolet, Johnson roared
through the distance around the
high-banked, quarter-mile of
asphalt in 6:30.24 to snap the
old mark of 6:31.48 set only
two weeks ago by defending
track champion Bill Lutz of
Louisville. It was also the first
O'Hare record to Johnson's
credit.
The finish of the 18-car grind
was a maze of 1957 Chevrolets
as the first four cars, all of
that make and year, took start-
er Art Kelly's checkered flag
with little more than inches
separating each mount. The
trailers behind Johnson were,
in order, Whitey Gerken of
Chicago, Lutz and Chicagoan
Roy Czach.
Action all evening was hot
and heavy with Roy Berry of
LaGrange, Ill., lucky to escape
unhurt after his '57 Chevy ca-
reened crazily off the back-
stretch wall on the first lap of
the feature. Berry wasn't hurt,
but his car was badly mangled

Walker pictures this situa-
tion in the form of a pyramid
with a car at its peak. The fate
of the car, he explains, involves
more and more people as the ef-
fect of its sales trickles down.
"In an automotive sense," he
says, "the American public de-
termines its own well-being.
The purchase of a car can start
a chain reaction that eventually
may lead to financial benefits
for the individual who bought
the car in the first place.
"As someone once pointed
out, we build cars by hundreds
of thousands, but we sell them
one at a time," Walker says.
"So it's as important to us as
it is to the prospective custom-
er that he likes what he sees."
Well-styled cars are one of
the best ways to spark public
interest and approval, according
to Walker.
"Far from being wasteful and
unnecessary,". he claims, "styl
ing innovations actually offer
other advantages to the public.
Favorable sales raise both buy-
ing and employment, help speed
technological .progress, encour-
age competition, and bring
enough used cars into the mar-
ket so that private transporta-
tion can be placed within reach
of just about everyone.
"I'm sure that explains why
the modern automotive stylist
has to be a realistic business
man as well as an artist,"
Walker adds. 'With so much at
