By Bob Horne
With wins in a heat race and
the fast-six already under his
belt, Don Boorse of Milwaukee
was far ahead of the field in the
25-lap main event when he blew
a tire with three laps remain-
ing, allowing the rapid Mel
Kenyon of Davenport, Iowa, to
chalk up his second feature
victory of the season.
Boorse bumped around on the
flat skin to finish the race in
the tenth spot.
Second-fast qualifier of the
evening, behind Mike Ladd of
Kenosha, Boorse had blasted his
Kutner No. 88 from the last
row, pole, into the lead with
but five laps gone. By mid-point
of the event, Don was far ahead
of the second-running Kenyon
and third-place Gary Congdon
of Whitewater in the beautiful
No. 50 Frederickson roadster
from Kenosha.
Billy Wood of Kenosha, this
season's point leader so far, had
mechanical trouble with his
usual ride, the Farchione No.
6 from Milwaukee, and took
over the No. 17 Studebaker or-
dinarily driven by Landy Scott
of Milwaukee. Running in the
fourth spot after 20 laps, Billy
again experienced bad luck and
had to pull out with more
trouble from the 17.
Kenyon was followed home
by Congdon, Duane Hanson of
Waterloo, and Al Fredenberg of
Madison.
The first heat event saw a
spirited scrap for the lead be-
tween Hal Tischendorf, Pista-
kee Bay, Ill., and John Hartwig
of Johnson Creek, Tischendorf
finally getting out front for the
win in the last half a lap. Bill
Yates, Cedar Rapids, Iowa,
finished third.
In the second heat Boorse got
his first win by charging from
the last row into second place
during the first lap and then
taking over the lead in the
seventh lap. Owen Snyder of
Janesville, who had shared the
last row with Boorse at the
start, also did a fine job of
motoring through traffic and
took a second, followed by Jack
Anderson of Racine.
The third heat went to Han-
son, who really bombed past the
field by moving from the third
row pole position into the lead
in a half lap after the green
flag dropped. Ladd and Frank
Connor, Milwaukee, had a good
hassle going for the second spot
