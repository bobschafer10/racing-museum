Frost Sets Record
By VER-BET
When his modified stock car
was first constructed probably
little thought was given to just
how far apart the wheels
should be, but last Saturday
nights performance by Jack
Frost, Zion, Ill., made the
builders' mechanical skills a
reality. The heavy rains Fri-
day night and early Saturday
morning at the Kenosha Coun-
ty Fairgrounds Speedway, Wil-
mot, Wis., left the track in a
condition which resembled the
Burma Road-after a World
War II aerial bombardment.
Management did what they
could in trying to remedy the
situation with some degree of
success but both corners re-
fused to yield to any changes--
the holes remained. Frost
jumped into the lead early in
the 25 lap feature and at top
speed, lap after lap, eased
his
modified power plant over
the
holes-—on side of the holes, by
the holes, but never in one of
the time consuming ruts and
his feats in maneuvering were
rewarding-top money plus a
new record, (52.05 mph). Jack
Radtke, Grays Lake, Ill., tried
everything in the books to close
the gap on Frost but the time
spent bouncing in the air made
the difference and second place
was his. Norm Nelson, current
leader in points from Racine,
Wis., found his abundant horse-
powers usuable on the straight-
aways but useless on the cor-
ners and had to settle for 3rd
place. The two heavy footed
gas pedal pushers from Wau-
kegan, Ill., Bill Shoulders and
Bob Karry waged a tight dual
for 4th and 5th places respec-
tively. The rains around the
Speedway which continued
through the evening, but by
some strange fate never
dropped on the track, caused
some rare fans to remain at
home, however there was no
shortage of drivers. The 15 lap
semi-feature looked like the
Outer Drive on a holiday week-
end. Thirty cars were sched-
uled, 27 appeared ready to run,
only 13 managed to miss the
holes-plus the traffic and com-
lete the event. Gordy Gorman,
Libertyville, Ill., wearing the
right pair of glasses, started
from the rear of the crowd of
eager fence busters and lap af-
ter lap threaded the needle to
his second victory of the eve-
ning (speed 49.76 mph). 2nd,
Roger Otto, Burlington, Wis.; be an upset.
