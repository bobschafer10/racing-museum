Twelve Enter
Hoosier Hundred
INDIANAPOLIS, IND.The
receipt of signed entry forms
today from Len Sutton of
Portland, Ore., and Gene Force
of Richmond, Ind., boosted the
official list to an even dozen
for the eighth annual Hoosier
Hundred USAC championship
race at the Indiana State Fair-
grounds on Saturday, Sept. 17.
Additional nominations will
be accepted until Sept. 9 and
Promoter Joe Quinn expects a
field of more than 25 to battle
for the 18 starting positions in
time trials beginning at 12:30
p.m. on race day. The race will
start at 3 p.m.
Force, a veteran of two 500-
mile Classics at the Indianapo-
lis Motor Speedway, made his
first Hoosier Hundred appear-
ance last year, but failed to
finish because of mechanical
trouble. In other 100-mile
events this season he placed
third at Langhorne, fifth at
Trenton and seventh at Mil-
waukee.
Sutton, who has driven in all
of the last three 500-mile races,
has appeared in two previous
Hoosier Hundred events. He
placed fourth in '57 and fifth
last year. His record on the
championship trail so far this
season includes a third-place
finish at Milwaukee.
Other entries already re-
ceived for the Sept. 17 event
at the Indiana State Fair
grounds have come from Rodg-
er Ward, Eddie Sachs, Tony
Bettenhausen, Jim Hurtubise,
Bobby Grim, Elmer George,
Parnelli Jones, Jim Hemmings,
Don
Freeland and Johnnie
Thomson. Ward and Sachs are
former Hoosier Hundred win-
ners.
Mail orders for tickets should
be addressed to the Hoosier
Hundred box office at the In-
diana State Fair grounds, In-
dianapolis, Ind. Box seats are
priced at $6, grandstand seats
at $5 and reserved bleacher
seats on the main straightaway
at $4, including federal tax
and general admission.

Beaver Dam Schedules
Another Modified "Go"
Because of overwhelming
success with its initial modi-
fied program on Aug. 15, Pro-
moters Hal Woods and John
Janczak have scheduled anoth-
er modified stock car program
at the Beaver Dam Raceway
on Monday night, Aug. 29. The
rain date is Monday night,
Sept. 12.
Miles Melius, Slinger, who
"cleaned house" on the 15th,
will head a field of some 50
cars and drivers, the fastest 40
of which will run for 40 per
cent. A consolation race for
non-qualifiers is also sched-
uled. Competition is open to all
comers with race-worthy cars
that are now competing on
stock and modified tracks
throughout this area.
The promoters have added a
new private road for pit per-
sonnel, allowing much quicker
access to the pit area. They
have also doubled all track
personnel to eliminate the long
delays in the ticket lines and
the parking problems experi-
enced in the first program. Ad-
ditional parking and seating
has also been arranged and the
concessions promise to be well
stocked for this event.
The management apologizes
for the long delay in starting
time trials on the first date,
but with added personnel and
improved facilities, the man-
agement promises the eight-
event program to start on time.
Qualifying rounds begin at
7 p.m. and the first event gets
under way at 8:15.
Radio station WBEV, 1430
kc, will broadcast the latest
track information if inclement
weather is prevailing.
