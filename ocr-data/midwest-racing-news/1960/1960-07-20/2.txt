By Louise Stahl
After traveling 140 miles
from Manitowoc to Griffith
Park Speedway in Wisconsin
Rapids, Bill Wood took the fea-
ture from Don Boorse by a half
car length.
Griffith Park is a new 1/5
mile asphalt track with some
of the asphalt no more than 24
hours old. This complicated
matters, because the track was
very slick and sandy.
The races were full of excite-
ment and mishaps. Jim Rice,
No. 51, took a slow roll in warm
ups. He spun in the turn and
when he hit the very soft sand
on the outside of the track, he
went into a very slow roll. He
wasn't hurt, but he might have
suffocated because his head was
completely buried in sand.
The sand was the biggest
problem of all. When sand
came onto the new asphalt, it
was worse than riding on ice.
The first heat got under way
and Jim Rice won it. He was
followed by Hal Tischendorf,
No. 17, Tom Krehl, No. 27, and
Gordy Edwards, No. 12.
Doug Mowry gave the sec-
ond heat some action when he
went too wide on the straight-
away. When he hit the soft
sand, he lost complete control
of his car. He careened across
the track just missing starter
Otto Stahl. He came to a stop
safely in the infield. The race
was won by Landy Scott, with
Dick Kincaid, No. 19, second,
and Gary Congdon, No. 50,
third.
Boorse took the third heat,
with Wood close on his trail.
They were followed by Jack
Joerns, No. 66, Tom Bigelow,
No. 14, and Mike Ladd, No. 4.

going out to sweep some of the
sand off, Boorse won the handi-
cap. Snyder was second with Al

biggest event in the history of

on West Washington St., be-

Gates will open at 6 p.m., with

Arsene J. Denoyer, chairman
of the Chilean Relief Commit-
tee for Lake County, reported
this week that $2,300 had been
raised in the county so far. Ad-
ditional contributions expected
from the Naval Training Center
and Fort Sheridan are expected
to at least double this amount.
Denoyer said, "We are still
far short of the $15,000 goal set
by the comimittee. But with gen-
erous offers such as this one
by Mr. Kaishian, we hope to
reach our goal. I earnestly urge
everyone in Lake County to
purchase tickets for this event.
Not only will you have an en-
joyable evening, but you will
also be doing a great deal to
help the suffering people of
Chile."
Late reports from Chile still
show unbelievable living con-
ditions for the two million
homeless from the earthquakes,
tidal waves, and volcanic erup-
tions that struck that country
on May 21. An urgent need for
cash contributions exists to pur-
chase the necessary tools and
supplies needed to complete the
tremendous' job of rebuilding.
