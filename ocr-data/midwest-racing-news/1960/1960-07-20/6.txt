MIDWEST RACING NEWS
Road Racing
By Robert "Sam" Linde
Sports car racing has become
big business in the United
States. This is evidenced by the
capacity crowds, crowds the size
of twenty-five, fifty, and hun-
dred thousand people, at such
tracks at Elkhart Lake, River-
side, Cumberland, and Sebring.
However, up until recently, all
sports car events were strictly
amateur. Such groups as the
Sports Car Club of America and
the California Sports Car Club
strived for a complete nonpro-
fessional atmosphere of racing.
Any amateur driver receiving
payment for racing was penal-
ized by suspension, and even
professional racing experience
in Europe was discouraged. For
some car owners and drivers
the expense of racing became
too much, and the spectator was
getting tired of seeing endless
production sports .car races
while waiting for the modified
or "big iron" to'start. In the
summer of 1958 the United
States Auto Club formed a road
racing division, designed to
bring active professionalism to
the sport. At first the SCCA
ruled that no driver could com-
pete professionally, and then in
the fall of 1958 SCCA allowed
professional competition as long
as the driver did not receive the
purse. As a result there is an
apparently ideal situation in
sports car racing. The active car
owner can be reimbursed by
racing professional, while the
production driver can still com-
pete in the numerous amateur
events. However, in the two
years that the USAC division
has been active, problems have
arisen.
The first professional sports
car races were held in the fall
of 1958 at various courses in the
East. These races lacked strong
fields and the spectator support
necessary to provide exciting
races with attractive purses.

INDIANAPOLIS, IND., July
18 Rodger Ward and Eddie
Sachs, two former winners of
the annual event, today became
the first official entrants for the
eighth running of the Hoosier
Hundred dirt track champion-
ship race on the one mile oval
at the Indiana State Fairgrounds
Saturday, September 17.
Ward, who now lives in In-
dianapolis, is the 1959 USAC
National champion and "500"
winner. He is the only driver
who has participated in every
Hoosier Hundred event, start-
ing in 1953, and his record at
the Fairgrounds includes two
third-place finishes in addition
to his triumph last September.
He'll be at the wheel of the
Leader Card Special with A. J.
Watson as his mechanic when
he tries to make it two-in-a-row
against an all-star field.
Sachs, who hails from Coop-
ersburg, Pennsylvania, set the
present Hoosier Hundred race
record of 92.142 miles an hour
with an impressive victory on
his first appearance in the event
two years ago.
He also won the poie position
for this year's "500" at the In-
dianapolis Motor Speedway by
10-
averaging 146.592 for the
mile test. On five different
oc-
casions during the first 200
miles of the race, he fought his
way into first place, but finally
was eliminated because of mag-

The chief mechanic on his

Hoosier Hundred will be Clint

Many other "500" veterans
and most of the leading money

Ticket orders should be ad-
