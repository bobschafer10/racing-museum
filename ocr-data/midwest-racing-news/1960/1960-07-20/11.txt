Three Keokuk,Iowa, drivers
dominated the action in the "Io-
wa International" at Des
Moines, but it was the "young-
ster" of the trio, Ramo Stott,
who walked away with the $1,-
050 first prize. Ernie Derr and
Dick Hutcherson led through
the early stages of the 300-lap
contest, but each, in his turn,
went to the sidelines to make
way for 26-year-old Stott's 1960
Ford convertible.
Al Sweeney and his National
Speedways crew had their
hands full fighting the ele-
ments. · A Saturday afternoon
downpour washed out time
trials and left the half-mile
Des Moines oval ankle deep in
mud. All Saturday morning the
track ws graded and ironed, but
by mid-race the coin turned
and blinding dust forced Swee-
ney to halt the contest at 150-
laps to wet down the surface.
Starting positions were deter-
mined by draw and Stott sat
23rd, well back in the pack.
Derr, who sat on the pole fol-
lowed by Hutcherson, held the
lead to the half way mark. The
"dust-stop" was a reprieve for
Derr whose rear-end gears
were gone. He changed during
the intermission and was on his
way, but 25 laps later went in
for keeps with engine trouble.
Hutcherson took over with a
full lap on Stott who had been
steadily improving his position.
With only 30 laps to go for
the checker, Hutcherson looked
like a cinch when his 1957 Pon-

hub and, like Derr, Dick went

money flag with a two lap
bulge
