Letters to the Editor
Thought you might like to print my version of the real story
behind the currently popular disk entitled, "Tell Laura I Love
Her." What more than likely happened is the following:
I needed cash, I needed fame,
To live up to my braggart's name,
To show my friends I wasn't nuts,
And show my gal that I had guts.
Her name is Laura!
3
She'd said if I had lots of cash,
For me she'd gladly "cook the hash,"
And so to save my garsh darn face,
I thought that I should run a race.
Just for Laura!
I bought a wreck, put roll bars in it,
Had it set within a minute,
Tested it for tops in speed,
I knew that I could take the lead.
And win for Laura!
And so one night, the big race came,
Fortune.awaited me-so did fame,
"I'll get out there and win hands down,
And win for me the champion's crown."
All for Laura!
I took my seat and strapped the door,
Pushed the pedal to the floor,
The body creaked, the engine rattled,
And through it all I really battled.
Please tell Laura!
I was way ahead, setting the pace,
Happiness shown about my face,
I was all alone, no one neared me,
I knew the others really feared me.
Please tell Laura!
Down the chute and around the bend,
I came upon the tail end
Of slower cars, for me to lap,
Already I heard the big crowd clap.
Oh, please tell Laura!
I must have provided each fan a thrill,
For the entire field was standing still,
And then I realized why the lag,
The starter hadn't dropped the flag.
Don't tell Laura!
I slammed on my brakes, stopping shortly after,
The crowd was screaming at me in laughter,
I felt so small, so far out of place,
I just withdrew from that dog-goned race.
Poor, poor Laura!
I'd let her down, but I felt great,
For I had learned a braggart's fate.
I'll blame it on the stars above her,
And please tell Laura I don't love her.
To H--- with Laura!
A Reader

Krieger Sweeps Waukegan
For Second Time!
Wins Third Consecutive Feature!
Riding the championship trail all the way, Gallant Greg
Krieger, ace modified stock car pilot from Milwaukee scored
his second consecutive clean sweep of events at the Waukegan
Speedway, Sunday night, June 26. In doing so, he won his third
consecutive feature event, scored once again in his ten lap heat
event and set the fastest qualifying speed for the fifth conse-
cutive time. The latter alone places his name in the books as
being the only driver able to accomplish this feat in the history
of the Waukegan track.
By virtue of his fastest time
of 51.05 mph., Gallant Greg
started in the rear of the fast
field of 18 cars and had quite
a battle on his hands as he wove
through the heavy, fast traffic.
Eddie Stillman, track champion
for the past two years, took ad-
vantage of a third row position
and grabbed the lead on the
second lap of the re-started
event. By the time Krieger
moved up into third position on
the ninth lap, Stillman seem-
ingly had the race in the bag
with a half lap lead over Krie-
ger. But Krieger kept pushing
and by the 15th, had moved
into second to begin his chal-
lenge of the leader.
Excitement raged as Krieger
kept closing the gap and on the
21st lap, the two were running
side by side. "Stillman encoun-
tered considerable trouble in
maneuvering the north turn,
running it wide and allowing
Krieger to move up. But on the
south turn, as Krieger was pass-
ing, and Eddie trying desper-
ately to maintain his lead, the
former champion spun out and
cut into the infield, losing his
position. He recovered in suffi-
cient time to continué in the
event and place second ahead of
Jack Frost of Zion, "Bay" Dar-
nell of Deerfield, Bob Rata-
jczyk of Kenosha, and Jack
Radtke of Grayslake. The re-
start came on the first lap as
Jack Frost spun out and was hit
by Gary Sheldon of Beloit,
blocking the north turn. Both
were able to restart again but
Sheldon lost a right rear wheel
on the restart and was forced
out of further competition.

Wednesday, June 29, 1960
July 4th Jalopies
At Waukegan
A new twist to current stock
car racing is going to be fea-
tured Monday night, July 4th,
at the Waukegan Speedway
when the track will be open to
strictly stock automobiles in an
action-packed race program ap-
propriately entitled "The July
Jalopy Jamboree." The added
program is being staged by pro-
moter John Kaishian in order to
fulfill the many requests for
such an event.
The program consists of heat
races, a semi, and a feature for
the jalopies and should provide
fans with countless thrills and
spills. Cars are limited to those
normally run in the amateur
and novice races as added at-
tractions at the various tracks.
They must be stock, have ade-
quate roll-over bars, three inch
safety belts properly secured, be
complete in-so-far as fire walls,
floor boards, fenders, hoods,
and the like are concerned, and
drivers must be 21 years old
and have an approved type rac-
ing helmet. In the event of rain,
the program will be rescheduled
for Tuesday night, July 5th.
A gigantic aerial display of
fireworks will climax the eve-
ning's action. Hot laps start at
7:30 and the races at 8:30. Ad-
mission is only $1 for adults,
with children under 12 years
of age admitted FREE.
