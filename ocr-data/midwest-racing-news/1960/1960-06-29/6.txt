By Al Krause
A sure bet for racing's Hall
of Fame and one of the most
popular drivers ever to appear
in Milwaukee is Melvin "Tony"
Bettenhausen, Tinley Park, Ill.
Bettenhausen's racing career
began at the old Chicago Ar-
mory midget races in Feb., 1938.
The Illinois Dutchman flipped
in his very.first race, something
he has done more than 30 times
in migetsand big cars but still
comes back to race again.
Tearing Tony will be 44 years
old in September but to watch
him drive you'd think he was
half that age. The sizzling Kraut
has qualified and started 14 In-
dianapolis 500 mile races and
has logged more miles in the
race than any other driver still
active. His total of more than
4,200 miles looks at though it'll
stand a long time in the current
crop of drivers.
Bettenhausen drove his first
race in Milwaukee in June, 1939
when he brought a fast little
Outboard-powered midget racer
here. By 1942, Bettenhausen
was winning features, so many
in fact that he was the fair park
midget champ that season. He
repeated those honors in 1946
and 1947. He also has won more
than a dozen other midget
championships.
Bettenhausen drove his first
big car race here in June, 1941,
in the famed blue No. 5 Iddlings
Hal Special. He finished fourth
in the 25 mile feature that day
behind Rex Mays, Duke Nalon,
and Tony Willman. That was
only the start of one of the most
illustrious big car racing ca-
reers in the business.
Since 1950, Tony has won
more than 20 national cham-
pionship speedway big car rac-
es, two of them at Milwaukee,
the Rex Mays Classics in 1950
and 1951. He has twice won the
national championship-in 1951
when he won eight of 13 starts
and in 1958 when he won the
title without having won a sing-
le big car race.
Since 1950 Bettenhausen has
won seven major racing events
here at the fair park. In 1950
and 1951 100 mile big car races.

Bettenhausen has had his
share of hard luck as well. In
1949 he was seriously burned
in a midget race at DuQuion,
Ill. And again in 1954 his mid-
get car, with the brakes gone,
climbed the outer wall at Sold-
iers Field, Chicago, dragging
him over 100 feet. He escaped
with a slight concussion and
cuts and bruises. Then in May,
1959, he lost control of the car
he was practicing with for the
500 mile race. The car hit the
outer wall, turned over and
skidded more than 200 feet.
Again Tony's luck held up and
he came out with cuts and
bruises.
At this writing Tearing Tony
ranks fourth in the all-time na-
tional big car point standings
behind Earl Cooper, Tommy
Milton, and Ralph DePalma.
Cooper has 13,695 points; Bet-
tenhausen, 10,795 points. An-
other couple of good seasons
and the Dutchman will be on
top of the heap. When asked
if he would retire if he won the
Indianapolis 500 mile race, still
his greatest ambition, Tony re-
plied, "Heck, no. I'd want to
prove to them I was a racing
champ."
In the off season Tony works
on his farm in Tinley Park,
with teen-age sons Merle and
Gary, helping run the equip-
ment. There are two other
youngsters, Susie and Tony Lee,
Valerie, his wife, who has
shared his racing fortunes and
heartbreaks for more than 20
years. Sunday, June 26, the
Dutchman takes another crack
at the sprinters and you can bet
he'll drive the Competition En-
gineering Special with all the
zip of a youngster on his way
up.
