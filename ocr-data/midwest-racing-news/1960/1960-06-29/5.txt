Milwaukee Sentinel
Outboard Marathon
FOND DU LAC, June 26
The roar of the nation's fastest
outboards was heard Sunday
in the 12th annual Milwaukee
Sentinel-Winnebagoland Mara-
thon. Many records were
broken.
New marks were hung up
in each of the five classes by
five new champions over the
smoothest water yet recorded
for this traditionally rough 88-
mile race. The winners:
Class D  Robert Jacobson,
Flint, Mich.; time 1:42.37 (51.45
miles per hour).
Class C  Joseph Schulte,
Palos Heights, Ill.; 1:53.44 (46.42
m.p.h.).
Class B-Bob Hering, She-
boygan; 1:57.24 (44.97 m.p.h.).
Class A - James Holden,
Trenton, Mich.; 2:23.51 (36.70
m.p.h.).
Class 36 Tullio Celano Jr.,
Elmsford, N. Y.; 2:13.53 (39.44
m.p.h.).
Schulte, 34-year old airline
pilot, gave the outstanding per-
formance of the day. He drove
his rig over the course 19 min-
utes and 40 seconds faster than
the old mark of 2:13.24. This
mark was set last year by Mi-
chael Harris of Garbill, Ind.
For his outstanding perfor-
mance, based on the best class
time in comparison to previous
time, Schulte received a new
boat.
Weather conditions were ideal
for the race. Despite an over-
cast sky, the southwest wind
failed to rise above 10 m.p.h.
This gave the drivers just rip-
ple on the water to give their
light craft a good lift.
The largest percentage of
drivers in the history of the
event finished the race; 85 out
of 134 starters.
Moberg, an early leader in
the race, was forced out when
the bottom fell out of his boat.
Craig Dewald, 20, Reading, Pa.,
was leading Class B to Oshkosh
when his boat hit the wake of a
spectator cruiser. He received a
cut in his knee when he was
flipped into the water and the
propeller caught his knee. He
was rescued by a patrol craft
and taken to Mercy Hospital
in Oshkosh where six stitches
were taken to close the cut. He
was released after treatment.
Dewald was the winner of the
Class A championship in 1954,
1955 and 1957.
Winner of the 1956 and. 1957
Class D competition, Jacobson,
again turned in the fastest time
in the speediest and largest of
the divisions. This makes Jacob-
son a three-time victor, dethron-
ing Ted Moberg of Wauwatosa,
champion of 1958 and 1959.
Jacobson's 5-year, old rig got
off to a fast start, was fourth
at the first checkpoint at Osh-
kosh, and held that position to
Wineconne. At the Fremont
turnabout Fred Samann of West
Allis was ahead of Jacobson by
15 seconds. This lead occurred
when Moberg dropped out.
The Michigan speedster, a
purchasing agent for a motor
plant, passed Samann on the
way back and from that time
on was never headed. He fin-
ished 48 seconds ahead of Gale
Cummings, Berkelye, Mich. and
Norman Schaub, Fond du Lac,
who was third.
