The information you request regarding point standing points
and paying points at the various tracks is too detailed to explain
through this column because, basically, each track operates their
particular point systems differently. However, each track can,
and will, supply drivers with a copy of their point systems simply
Line-ups are figured according to time trials, with the first
two heats run under straight time, inverted starts, and the third
and fourth heats staggered: i.e. with 40 carsslowest 10 in the
first heat, next slowest 10 in the second, and top 20 staggered
in the third and fourth, meaning that the second, fourth, sixth,
etc., qualifiers run the third heat, and the first, third, fifth,
At most tracks every entrant is allowed to run at least one
heat race. However, as a general rule, the top 18 cars run t
the
feature, and the semi-feature is limited to 24 cars. This is the
only determining factor insofar as qualifying or not qualifying
is concerned. Consequently, under that system, only the top 42
cars qualify for either race and over and above 42 automatically
eliminates the slower drivers from the semi feature. Mechanical
difficulties can also eliminate those who qualify for either race
from competing, thus moving up slower cars that would not
As a further example, if a feature car "scratches" (unable
to compete because of mechanical troubles, etc.), the last position
in the semi automatically takes the pole position in the feature
to make up a full starting field. If 24 starters are scheduled in
the semi and one "scratches," the next qualifier, who normally
would have been eliminated, takes the pole position in the semi.
This, of course, transpires only if officials are notified on time.

According to several track officials, stroking is an age-old

Wednesday, August 17, 1960
SCCA
at Daytona
The new sports car course at
Daytona International Speed-
way, designed mostly for the
smaller cars and Formula Ju-
nior machines for the SCCA
regional races Saturday and
Sunday, Sept. 3 and 4, meas-
ures 1.63 miles and add a third
selection for the sports car driv-
ers to use in competing at the
world famous speedway.
William C. France, track su-
perintendent, and Joe Epton,
assistant and NASCAR chief
scorer and timer, completed the
measuring of the new course
and came up with the 1.63 mile
figure after an original estimate
of 1.7 miles.
The new course will provide
more curves and "U" turns than
either the 3.1 or 3.81 mile sports
car courses, but eliminates both
of the high-banked east and
west turns.
Six races are scheduled for
Sunday, Sept. 4, these including
two 10-lap races over the 1.63
mile course, one 10-lapper over
the 3.1 mile course, two 25-lap
races over the 1.63 mile course
one over the 3.1 mile
and
course.
The races over the 1.63 mile
course are designed mainly for
the smaller cars and also for
the Formula Junior machines
while the longer course that in-
cludes most of the two and a
half mile speedway with the ex-
ception of the west banked turn
will be used for the larger cars.

chairman of the race program,
