Waukegan Feature
To Miles Melius
Schwister Sets Fast Time; Krieger Stopped!
By Jim Engel
WAUKEGAN, August 14 Before a small frost-bitten crowd
of only 1,850 persons, Miles Melus, the mighty modified king
from Slinger, Wisconsin, making hs first appearance of the year
at the Waukegan Speedway, captured top honors in winning
the 25 lap main event for the modifieds. He also set a new 10
lap track record in winning hs preliminary heat race. The race
hungry fans also saw Milwaukeean Bill Schwister set the fast
qualifying time of the evening when he turned the quarter-mile
oval at speed of 54.05 mph. Schwister's performance stopped
Greg Krieger, also of Milwaukee, from another streak .of fast
times. Krieger has already posted top honors in that class, 10
heat event by only a matter of
inches over Greg Krieger, in a
the
real thriller of the evening.
A special added attraction, a
stock jalopy race with the six
Miller brothers of Gurnee, pro-
vided fans with some real ac-
tion packed thrills. Virge Miller
took the top honors for being
the roughest driver on the
track. Brother Bob Miller was
the victor at an average speed
of 44.96 mph. But it was quite
obvious to the fans that all
the Miller boys with the excep-
tion of winner Bob, were try-
ing for the roughest trophy
award. Brother Jack rolled over
one complete time, brother Don
was pushed into the wall, as
was Sonny, and Cliff and Virge
ended up in an infield ditch,
before the action was
over.
Promotor John Kaishian
then
awarded all five an identical
award, with Bob taking the win
trophy.
Only seven cars out of a
field of 21 starters finished the
stock jalopy 12 lap feautre. Ed
Jones, Waukegan survived the
numerous spin-outs and crashes
to come home the victor.
Next week in addition to the
usual number of events for the
modifieds and stock jalopies,
track manager Mike Kaishian
will stage a special race for
members of the Coca Cola com-
pany. It will be Coca Cola Em-
ployees night at the track next
week and Kaishian has lined
up several of the employees to
drive in a special race all their
own. Still one other attraction
will be held, and that being the
repeatedly requested Demoli-
tion Derby, where strictly stock
autos will be deliberately de-
molished. Time trials will begin
at 7:15 with the first race be-
ginning at 8:30.

MIDWEST RACING NEWS
1960 Fair Park Season
Officially Closed
The 1960 season of modified
stock car racing on the quar-
ter-mile track at the Wisconsin
State Fair Park, Milwaukee has
officially closed as of Thursday
night, August 1lth. The early
close was made necessary to
make way for the opening of
the Wisconsin State Fair
on
August 19.
The final tally in point stand-
ings at the Fair Park show
Billy Johnson, Jr., Oconomowoc
and
Etchie Biertzer, Barton,
tied for the top honors with
1,920 points each.
Billy Schwister, Milwaukee
placed third with 1,775 points,
but was a hardluck. victim in
Thursday's 100 lap champion-
ship feature race that would
have earned for him the sea-
son's undisputed championship
title. Schwister's car caught
fire on the 69th lap of the event
as he was leading the race,
dropping him from contention.
Despite four rainouts during
the season, the speedway still
wound up one of its better
years. Total purses for the nine
dates held amounted to $18,070,
the average purse amounting to
$2,007 per night.
The speedway plans reopen-
ing the latter part of May, 1961.
Brabham Wins
Fifth Straight
Australian Jack Brabham
won the Portuguese Gran Prix
Sunday in Oporto, Portugal.
This was the fifth straight vic-
tory for last year's world cham-
pion and practically assures
him of the championship again
this year. Brabham was driv-
ing a Cooper-Climax as was
second place, New Zealand's
Bruce McLaren. Both cars are
sponsored by the Cooper fac-
tory. Third was Jim Clark in a
Lotus, and fourth, in the first
non-British car to finish, was
Wolfgang von Trips in a Fer-
rari.
