Hoosier Hundred
INDIANAPOLIS, IND.The
official entry list for the eighth
annual Hoosier Hundred big
car championship race at. the
Indiana State Fairgrounds on
September 17 grew to 10 today
with the addition of two Hoos-
ier drivers.
Bobby Grim of Indianapolis
will be in the cockpit of the
Elmer
Forbes Special and
George of Speedway, Indiana,
will be at the wheel of the
HOW Special.
Earlier entries received by
Promoter Joe Quinn include
Rodger Ward, Tony Betten-
hausen, Eddie Sachs, Jim Hurt-
ubise, Johnnie Thomson, Don
Freeland, Parnelli Jones and
Jim Hemmings. Fifteen or 16
additional nominations are ex-
pected and the 18 fastest quali-
fiers will form the starting line-
up on the one-mile dirt oval.
It will be the fourth Hoosier
Hundred appearance for
George, the 1957 Midwest
sprint car champion and win-
ner of the Syracuse 100-mile
championship event in 1958.
Grim, former IMCA cham-
pion who won "Rookie of the
Year" honors at the Indianap-
olis Motor Speedway in 1959,
finished sixth in his first Hoos-
ier Hundred race last Septem-
ber. He also was among the
championship point winners at
Trenton earlier this year and
in the starting lineup again for
the "500" on May 30.
Tickets for the September 17
race already are on sale at the
Indiana Fairgrounds and mail
orders should be addressed to
the Hoosier Hundred box of-
fice at the Fairgrounds, Indian-
apolis 5, Indiana. Box seats are
priced at $6, grandstand seats
at $5, and reserved bleacher
seats on the main straightaway
at $4, including federal tax and
general admission.

Melius Sweeps Program
At Beaver Dam
Record Attendance and Purse Set
Before an all-time record
crowd of an estimated 4,250
persons, Miles Melius of Sling-
er, Wisconsin, swept the field
at the Beaver Dam Raceway
Monday night, August 15, as
the raceway staged its first
modified stock car "go" of the
season.
Acclaimed a tremendous suc-
cess by the promoter, Hal
Woods, plans are in the making
to hold a similar program
sometime again this year.
Not knowing what the re-
sults were to be in staging a
modified program, Woods had
guaranteed the drivers a purse
of over $1,000 for the "off
night" run. In view of the at-
tendance, however, and at the
requests of some of the drivers,
Woods upped the ante to a flat
40 per cent, and drivers shared
in a record prize purse cf $2,240.
Boasting a field of some 55
cars and drivers, the fastest 40
qualified for the heats, semi,
and feature. Non-qualifiers ran
in a special consolation race
after the feature event.
In qualifying, Melius turned
the track in 17.99 seconds, an
all-time one lap record, with a
measured speed of 50.03 mph.
Feature action was evident
from the drop of the flag. Greg
Krieger, Milwaukee, powered
his way to the number one spot
by the third lap, taking over
from Joe Doeraty of Rio. From

time and wound up eighth in
the final tally.
