MEADOWDALE
When the Meadowdale sports
car course first opened it be-
came a controversial course.
The first race was uncomfort-
able to both the participant and
the spectator. Actually, the
Meadowdale situation was not
relieved last year, as they only
held three USAC professional
road races. Many bad accidents
happened, both Art Bunker and
Ricardo flipped Porsches, neith-

of a driver, and last September
Jim Rathmann flipped his Lis-

the situation now appears dif-
ferent. Several small sports car
races have been held, and re-
cently the Milwaukee region
hosted an SCCA National Race
at Meadowdale. In many ways
this was the coming-in of Mea-
dowdale as a good course. Even
though the course is still "squir-
relly," the drivers have learned
to take it easy, and the experi-
enced drivers who know the
course like it. Drivers such as
Ed Walsh, Curt Gonstead, arid
Vern Toulon commented that it
was a challenging course, but
enjoyable. Although there were
minor prangs and bumps to cars

LATE MODEL STOCKS
The first late model stock car
race of the year was the 200
mile event at the Milwaukee
one mile asphalt track. This
year's race attracted an excep-
tional field of new automobiles,
many of them in immaculate
condition. Standouts were the
Norm Nelson 1960 Ford, the Ray
Nichels-Paul Goldsmith Pon-
tiac, Bill Cheesbourg's '59 Ford,
the East Side Chevrolet driven
by Jim Rathmann, and John E.
Roestek's new Ford. Before the
race there was some conflict
about letting Roestek run. This
year Roestek has been compet-
ing occasionally in NASCAR
late model races, and USAC did
not want him to change organi-
zations. However, the USAC
officials finally let Roestek run.
In spite of being involved in
several accidents, Roestek was
able to finish tenth.
The start of the race was
beautiful, and Goldsmith's ride
out front was marvelous to
watch. This definitely was the
fastest combination at State Fair
Park, Goldsmith, Nichels, Pon-
tiac. The last big stock car win
for Goldsmith was at the 1958

has always been a consistent
