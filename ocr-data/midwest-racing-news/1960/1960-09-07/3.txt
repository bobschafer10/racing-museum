men know their jobs and then
do them."

director Tom Marchese, a short,
heavy set man of Italian ex-
traction who has a positive gen-
ius for organization. For the

Cudahy, took third place mon-

Through the years, Marchese
has concentrated on building
attendance and prize money.
But the other segments of his
well-oiled machine have not
been neglected. To assure the
drivers and fans of quick re-
sults, Marchese has developed
scoring system that has
a
proved faster and more accu-

tracks. Two chief scorers han-

tabulating the results of each
race that are announced before
the fans have left the grand-
stand.
The fire and
emergency
crew work is another well-han-
dled operation with many of
the members of this crew em-
ployed as firemen in their ev-
eryday job. The fire chief of a
large Milwaukee firm acts as
fire chief and a former race
driver with many years of ex-
perience handles the emergen-
cy crew. In addition, Marchese
had two ambulances, two emer-
gency trucks, two jeeps, a
pumper fire truck, and more
than 200 fire extinguishers
varying in weight from 10 to
250 pounds.
To make sure each official
is doing his job and is sta-
tioned where he should be,
Marchese has equipped them
with shirts. The chief of each
committee or group wears a
white shirt. The lemon yellow
shirts are for technical commit-
tee members while the red
shirts are for fire and emer-
gency squads.

Through the years, the fans
who see the races at the fair
park come to regard these
smoothly run races as a matter
of course. Little do they know
that over a long period of time
Marchese has selected the men
and trained them for these jobs.
Including the scoring staff,
nearly 200 men are engaged in
the handling of each major rac-
ing event at the fair park. This
ranges all the way from Chief
Zone Supervisor Chet Barne-
kow to Tom DuPont and Bu-
ford Harding who circle the
track handing out water to the
officials and Tom Marchese
considers each man to be of
equal importance when he is
doing his job.
This is just one more reason
why Milwaukee can be called
the one mile auto racing center
of America.
