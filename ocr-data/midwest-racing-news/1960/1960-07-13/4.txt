By Al Krause
Not only is Milwaukee's fa-
mous one mile paved oval the
scene of the most national
championship races each year,
but it is also equipped with one
of the largest staffs of trained
officials that will be found any-
where, complete with a full line
of fire and emergency equip-
ment.
Through the years, promoter
Tom Marchese has purchased
this equipment out of his own
pocket. Many drivers say it is
a source of great comfort to
them to know that the best pro-
tection possible is being given
to them.
Many people think that the
races at Milwaukee that come
off without a hitch just happen.
They couldn't be further from
the truth Marchese through the
years has developed and trained
of more than 150 men
not include the 70 members who
run the scoring setup, a setup
that has proved to be much fast-
er and far more accurate than
that employed at any other
track in the country.
At each race, either on the
big or small track, there are
seven vehicles owned and oper-
ated by Wisconsin Auto Racing,
Inc. They include two each of
ambulances, emergency trucks,
and jeeps, as well as one large
fire truck.
Each of the ambulances is fully
equipped with the necessary
first ald and oxygen equipment.
Two physicians, a first aid as-
sistant and a nurse are at track-
side before a wheel is turned on
race day. Two emergency trucks
are handled by men experienced
in this work. The same for the
pair of jeeps.
Through the years, Marchese
has developed the firefighting
equipment and is constantly
striving for methods to improve
it. Several regular firemen and
volunteer firemen operate the
fire fighting equipment. Mar-
chese stated that there are 25
thirty pound CO2 extinguishers,
40 twenty pound dry chemical,
and six fifty pound CO2 extin-
guishers.
There is also a fire truck
purchased from a small down-
state town with a 500 gallon
capacity rigged with a special
fogging nozzle. As a standby,
the Wisconsin State Fair park
has a 2,000 gallon sprinkling
truck that can pump water to
the fogging truck.
