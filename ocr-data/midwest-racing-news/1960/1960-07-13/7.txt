The first set of races held on
Sunday, July 3rd, was marred
by the death of MGA driver Ed-
ward Gerver. In fact the pro-
duction races were run with
little care of safety. During the
E production race a Triumph

of the concrete posts of the dam.
These accidents were partly the
fault of the course, a course that
is run over a dam and several
bridges. The escape roads are
inadequate and many trees are
located where spinning cars
could quite easily hit them. Al-
so, it seemed that the drivers
had not learned proper respect
for this potentially dangerous
course. Following Hinkle in the

Romeo to finish. This and the
F production race were held up
because of restarts necessary
after several bad accidents.

is'set by Burt Ross, Jr., Spokane, Wash., salesman, as he pilots
this hydroplane powered by a production 60-cubic-inch Mercury
with just the lower half of the
propeller biting below waterline.

tional Outboard Association's
spéed mark of 114.6 mph set
recently by a specially-modifed
90-cubic-inch engine which had
50 per cent .greater displace-

Mercury. The NOA unlimited
class speed marks, not recog-

the United States are serviced
by cast iron water and gas
mains still in use after 100
years, according to Thomas F.
Wolfe, managing director of the

sociation, Chicago.
Municipal and privately
owned water and gas supply

continuous use for more than a
hundred years are members of
the Cast Iron Pipe Century
