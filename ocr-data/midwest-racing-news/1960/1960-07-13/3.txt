Wednesday, July 13, 1960
By Jim Engel
He didn't even know he had
won. That's how close and com-
petitive the main event for the
modified stocks at the Fair Park
Speedway was Thursday night,
July 7, as Miles Melius, Slinger,
Wisconsin, came home the vic-
tor after a bitter, gruelling grind
of 30 laps around the fast quar-
ter mile track. The program was
the first in four weeks after the
local track had experienced
three consecutive Thursday
night rainouts.
Eighty cars (50 modifieds, 22
amateurs, 8 demolition) and
drivers participated in the eve-
nings program and provided
fans with every racing thrill
imaginable as speed, close com-
petition, and skillful driving, all
but over-shadowed the spins,
crashes and roll-overs. Fans
were treated to a feature race,
the likes of which have seldom,
if ever, been seen before at the
quarter-mile racing center.
Action was furious almost
from the start of the event and
continued on right "down to the
wire." As the 18 fastest cars
roared into lap two, Billy John-
son, Oconomowoc, was hit by
Dale Koehler, Glendale, sending
the latter airborne some 20 feet.
He "landed" perfectly and both
cars continued on without loss
of position. Lap number 3 and
a four car pile-up took Eddie
Stillman, Milwaukee, Fuzzy
Fassbender, Slinger, Norbie
Klemp, Fredonia, and Bill
Strom, West Allis, out of the
contention for a top spot. On lap
number 6, Carl Kulow, Ply-
mouth, making a strong bid for
third position, spun out and was
chalked up as another circum-
stantial victim.
Norm Nelson Stars
"Bay" Darnell, Deerfield, Ill.,
winner of the season opener on
May 26, was holding the lead
at this time, but veteran stock
car pilot, Norm Nelson, Racine,
kept moving up steadily and by
lap number 12 had taken over
the number one position. Right
behind him and in order were
Greg Krieger, Milwaukee; Mel-
ius; Bill Schwister, Milwaukee;
Johnson; Etchie Biertzer, Bar-
ton; and Koehler. From here
through the 25th lap it was a
nip and tuck battle with all po-
sitions but Nelson's changing
hands several times.

Melius Nearly Rolled!
While the fans were tingling
with excitement over the photo-
finish feature, they vividly re-
called the feverish moments of
the third heat race when Mel-
ius tangled with Freddie Rey-
nolds, Milwaukee, and nearly
rolled over. Reynolds had spun
directly in front of him and
Melius turned sharply to avoid
a serious mishap. As he did so,
he tipped, and rode up and over
the hood of Reynold's car. Mel-
ius' car rode some 15 to 20 feet
with the left side facing sky-
ward at about a 60 degree angle
to the ground. Fortune rode
with Melius as he "poured the
coals" to the Corvette powered
Ford and settled to earth once
again, continuing on to a fourth
place in the event.
In the fourth heat Ken Tlou-
gan, Milwaukee, set a new 10
lap track record when he aver-
aged 51.90 mph in winning. The
old mark of 51.28 mph was set
June 9, 1960 by Stillman.
Amateur Feature Halted Twice
Twenty-one cars started in
the 15 lap novice feature for
strictly stock cars and only eight
finished the full 15 laps, as seri-
ous roll-over accidents and
other crashes halted the event
two different times. Ron Wolfe,
Milwaukee, flipped on the sec-
ond lap landing in the middle of
the track just off the north turn.
Mass confusion resulted and on
the restart only sixteen cars
were able to continue. On lap 8,
Roy Schmatzhagen, Milwaukee,
rolled over 2½2 times, landing
on his roof on the main straight
away. As the red flag came
down for the second time, Cal-
vin Weck, Milwaukee, spun and
rolled two times on the south
turn and Darrell Pentico, Wau-

Nelson—White
Milwaukee 200
Don White, Keokuk, Ia., and
Norm Nelson, Racine, Wis., con-
tinue their battle for the lead
in the United States Auto Club
late model national champion-
ship stock car standings in the
200 mile stock car race at the
one mile paved oval at State
Fair Park, Sunday, July 24.
Both will be in 1960 Fords.
White won the 300 lap race at
Nashville, Tenn., early in June,
Nelson finished second. Nelson
came back to win the 200 lap
grind at Columbus, Ohio on
July 4. Going into the race here,
White has 300 points, Nelson
White and Nelson will drive
two of the five 1960 Fords that
are among the 14 entries re-
ceived so far. In all, 11 drivers
have entered in Fords. Other
'60 Fords will be driven by Bill
Shoulders, Waukegan, Ill.; Bill
Cheesbourg, Tucson, Ariz., and
Sol Tovella, Chicago.
Nelson, whose car is owned
by Mil w a u kee businessman,
William Trainor, has won four
major stock car races at State
Fair Park. He is tied with Mar-
shall Teague, Daytona Beach,
Fla., for the most races won
here on the big track.
Other entries are Allen Swen-
son, Glenview, Ill. (1958 Ford);
Al Shear, Rockford, Ill. (1958
Ford); Gordon Gorman, Liber-
tyville, Ill. (1958 Ford); Mike
Klapak, Warren, Ohio (1958
Ford); Ed Kozbiel, Chicago
(1958 Ford); Darrell Dake, Ce-
dar Rapids, Iowa (1958 Chev-
rolet); and Marvin Roath, Lom-
bard, Ill. (1958 Plymouth).
Race director Tom Marchese
is confident he'll have more
than 45 entries by deadline time.
The fastest 40 qualifiers will
start the race. Time trials at
12 noon; race lines up at 2 p.m.,
central daylight savings time.
