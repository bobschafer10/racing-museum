=== COLUMN 5 ===
187.50
By Robert "Sam" Linde
Art Malone driving the Don
Garlit's "Swamp Rat" out of
Tampa, Fla., and Chris Kara-
mesines in the "Chizler" from
Chicago, both turned in identi-
cal track record breaking speeds
of 187.50 miles an hour at Union
Grove, Wisconsin. This was an
appropriate climax to a full
three days of national cham-
pionship drag racing. Actually,
the speeds these cars turned are
not surprising considering that
both the "Swamp Rat" and the
"Chizler" have turned speeds
beyond 200 miles an hour under
ideal conditions. Both of these
cars run GMC super-chargers on
highly modified Chrysler en-
gines and burn pure nitrometh-
ane or "fuel." Many records
were broken on Sunday, one of
the most sensational being the
run of Jim Leineweber, Chi-
cago, in a Harley-Davidson mo-
torcycle. Leineweber was able
to coax 137.41 miles an hour at
the end of the quarter mile. This
speed is more than 13 miles an
hour over the previous record.
Bud Thompson and Mike Mari-
noff of Milwaukee were also
record breakers. Thompson
broke the record in B Street
Roadsters in his 1957 Chevrolet
powered 1932 Ford coupe, while
Marinoff bettered his record
driving the Hall Chevrolet in
the B Sedan and Coupe class.
The three days of dragging
came to an end Sunday with a
capacity crowd estimated at
8,00o. Next Sunday Union Grove
returns to its normal weekly
schedule with most of the en-
trants coming from the immedi-
ate area.
Several cars that had the rep-
utation of turning in fantastic
s pe e ds proved disappointing.
Art Arfors' "Green Monster,"
a
dragster powered by an Alliçon
aircraft engine turned in a rec-
ord speed of 166 miles an hour
Saturday. On Sunday Arfons
made one run and then packed
up and went home with mech-
anical difficulties. However, his
speed was the fastest "gas"
speed turned during the meet.
The "Lone Ranger," a dragster
using a Ranger aircraft engine
never did make a successful
run. One of the most consistent
cars of the meet was the "Big
Wheel," from Minneapolis,
Minn. This car always turned
in speeds of 169 miles an hour
and appeared to be immaculate-
ly prepared.
