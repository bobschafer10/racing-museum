By Bob Horne
So intent were they on their
battle for the lead, which had
been raging for 27 torrid laps,
that Billy Wood of Kenosha and
Keith Thomas of Madison
looked something like a pair of
old milkwagon nags wearing
side-blinders when Owen Sny-
der of Janesville, with the fast
heat already under his belt,
quite suddenly blasted out in
front on the fortieth lap and
went on to win the special 50-
lap feature event at Angell
Park last Sunday night.
Nobody would ever attempt
to detract from the abilities of
Wood and Thomas, but the
crowd roared as much with
amusement as excitement when
Snyder pulled his coup, obvi-
ously unexpected by either of
the veteran chargers he blew
Fresh from a Saturday night
feature victory at Cedar Rap-
ids, Iowa, in his twin team car,
the Chief Automotive Offy out
of Madison, Owen pushed
the
Ford counterpart around
the
three-tenths oval about
as
quickly as he could have done
with the Growler.
For many laps-since 13 to
be preciseThomas had
breathed down Wood's neck,
apparently waiting for the
opening to get through. The
track was rough this night and
nobody went anywhere except
down on the pole. But during
that split second when the
opening did occur, Snyder came
along from out of nowhere
(wouldn't that make a fine song
title?) and there he was out
front. It was something of an
anticlimax when Thomas final-
ly got around Wood on lap 45.
Finishing fourth behind Wood
was the very rapid Falcon-pow-
ered ride of George Sellary,
Chicago, who on this, his first
outing at Sun Prairie, qualified
sixth fastest and went on to win
the hotdog event for those six
fastest cars of the evening. Mel
Kenyon, Davenport, Ia., winner
of the previous week's main
event, finished fifth, followed
by Billy Horstmeyer, Madison,
in sixth.
The Whitewater Brigade
made things interesting as the
show got under way with Tom
Bigelow and Dick Jones, both
from the state college town, fin-
ishing one-two. Bigelow really
planted his boot as the green
flag fell and bombed from his
fourth row starting slot into
second in one lap, followed by
Jones, who owns the car Bige-
low drives. Five laps later, Big-
elow got around Hal Tischen-
dorf, Pistakee Bay, Ill., and in-
to the lead. With but a half-lap
remaining, Jones outmaneu-
vered Tischendorf in the turn
and went into second before the
finish line.
