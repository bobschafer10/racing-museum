Rathmann Heads List of
Fair Speed Week Entries
by Al Krause
MILWAUKEE, WIS., Aug. 7
-All roads lead to Milwaukee
for the biggest paying week of
auto racing in the country, as
the nation's top auto racers
prepare for four big days of
racing during the annual Wis-
consin State Fair, Aug. 19-28.
Race director Tom Marchese
stated that 1960 500 mile race
winner Jim Rathmann, Miami,
Fla., heads the list of more than
25 entries for the big national
championship events.
Marchese estimates that more
than 75,000 fans will see the
races. There will be two late
model stock car events-Sun-
day, Aug. 21-150 miles and
Thursday, Aug. 25, 200 miles.
Saturday, Aug. 27, the mighty
midgets compete in the 100
mile thriller. Sunday, Aug. 28
is the big one the 200 mile
national championship speed-
way big car grind. Prize money
for the big car grind is ex-
pected to run over the $35,000
mark.
The guarantee for the mid-
get grind has been raised from
$5,000 to $6,000 by Marchese.
Along with the $1,000 put up
by the Firestone Tire Co., this
assures the midgeteers of their
biggest payday of the season,
as $7,000 will be assured. If
the crowd is larger and the 40
per cent of the net gate goes
past the $6,000 mark, the driv-
ers stand to make even more.
Marchese feels total prize mon-
ey for the week will run to
around $70,000.
Rathmann has indicated he'll
drive the same Ken-Paul Spec-
ial he used in his 500 mile race
triumph. He heads the field of
17 entries for the big car grind.
Others are Eddie Sachs, Center
Valley, Pa. (Dean Van Lines);
Johnny Thompson, Boyertown,
Pa. (Hoover Motor Lines Spl.);
Parnelli Jones, Torrance, Calif.
(Braund Plywood Spl.); Gene
Force, Richmond, Ind. (Tiz-So
Spl.), and Jack Rounds, River-
side, Calif. (Hart Fullerton
Spl.). Thomson has moved out
of the Racing Associates Spec-
ial and it is expected Jim Hur-
tubise, Lennox, Calif. may be
in that car for the race.
Other topflight big car en-
tries are Jack Turner, Seattle,
Wash. (King O'Lawn spl.); Don
Freeland, Speedway, Ind. (John
Zink Spl.); Jim Packard, In-
dianapolis (Steerly Motor Lines

Leep 1st at Memphis
Yaeger Killed
Track records and tragedy
marked the return of IMCA
sprint car racing to the Scot-
land County Fair in Memphis,
Mo., after a ten year lapse.
The one-lap record was last
established by Frank Luptow
of Tampa, Fla., a decade ago at
28.20. Before Friday's time trials
were over 14 cars had broken
or tied the standing mark. Har-
old Leep of Wichita went home
with the track standard tucked
in his hip pocket when he
turned a 25.04 lap to nose Pete
Folse out of the number one
starting position. Folse, well
below the old mark, had turned
in a sensational 25.33 earlier.
Floyd Yaeger, veteran modi-
fied and midget pilot from Pitts-
field, Ill., received fatal injuries
when his left front spindle
broke during the running of the
consolation race. Yaeger went
over the bank and rolled three
times. He was dead on arrival
at Davis County Hospital in
n ear by Bloomfield. Yaeger
owned a garage and the Pitts-
field Speedway.
Leep jumped into the feature
lead at the drop of National
Speedway starter Al Sweeney's
green flag and was never head-
ed, holding a commanding lead
for the 20 laps. Folse finished
second in the Hector Honore
Bardahl Spl. Trailing Leep and
Folse were Gordon Woolley and
Sonny Helms. Time for the dis-
tance was 8:30.65.
Heat winners were Leep,
Folse, Helms and Frogie Droz.
Folse won the five-lap match
race from Leep and Hugh Ran-
dall.
