Wednesday, August 10, 1960
Schwister Takes First
Hales Corners
program of the season.
Bill Schwister averaged 56.21
mph in capturing his first ma-
jor Hales Corners victory of the
season before 3,750 enthusiastic
fans, and Greg Krieger became
the first driver in the speed-
way's eleven year history to
break 60 mph as he set a new
one-lap track record in qualify-
ing runs at the average speed
of 60.07 mph. The former rec-
ord was held by Etchie Biertzer
of Barton at 59.93 mph and set
on July 23 of this year.
Schwister took the lead on
the 13th lap, taking over from
Bob Ratajczyk of Kenosha, and
went on to a three car length
margin victory over veteran
Miles Melius of Slinger. Melius,
however, turned in a tremen-
dous performance in both speed
and driving. By the eighth lap
Melius had moved into sixth
spot when he locked wheels
with Norbie Klemp, Fredonia,
and the two cars ran into a
large "pond" of water on the
infield backstretch, sending
forth a shower of steam and
water spray (the water had ac-
cumulated from recent rains).
Klemp's car stalled out in the
center of the "pond," but Mel-
ius charged through.
In the mishap he lost several
spots and was running in a
near-last position when he fi-
nally regained control and got
back on the track. From this
point on there was no stopping
the Mighty Modified King.

though they were standing still,
and had the race been a few
laps longer, there isn't much

Win
Krieger Breaks 60 MPH In Qualifying
By Jim Engel
HALES CORNERS, AUGUST 6-Two fellow Milwaukeeans
were the stars in Hales Corners Speedway action Saturday night,
August 6, as the modified stock cars roared through their tenth
win. Melius' speed while mov-
ing through the traffic was so
noticeably faster than usual, it
prompted scorer Eugene
Sweeney to put the clock on
him for one lap, and the results
showed an unofficial one-lap
track record of 17.32 reconds or
60.62 mph (the official record
for one lap is still held by
Krieger for the record he set
during qualifying runs).
In the fourth 10-lap heat Mel-
ius was slowed by an entangle-
ment with Schwister, but came
up through the field to take a
last lap victory over Krieger
with a speed of 56.94 mph.
Herman Roggendorf, Mil-
waukee, survived a near end-
overend flip on the third lap
of the second heat to go on for
the win. He tangled wheels
with Homer Ansell of Milwau-
kee on the backstretch, and
rode up and over the wheels,
landing nose first, but contin-
uing on with no noticeable loss
of time.
Jerry Breske of Milwaukee
was the hard-luck driver of the
night. After thoroughly demol-
ishing his old automobile in the
Cedarburg, Wisconsin opening
race event May 18, Breske
spent all this time building a
new
machine and Saturday
night was its initiation. Fresh
from the paint shop and in
beautiful condition, Breske lost
control in the first heat event
and flipped the machine end-
over-end, jumping a concrete
retainer wall on the main
stretch, landing in the infield
just four feet from the judges'
stand. Shaken, but otherwise

Minot Windup
To Pete Folse
Pete Folse, defending IMCA
sprint car titlist, became the
top money winner of the 1960
North Dakota State Fair by
winning the 15-lap finale in
Minot. A cracked steering hous-
ing put Folse's chief rival, A. J.
Shepherd, out of action in the
dash and left the door open for
Pete's run to the feature
checker.
Harold Leep finished second
in the Auto Racing, Inc. event
with Buzz Barton third. Bill
Hobbs and LeRoy Neumayer
closed in a dead heat for fourth
position. Heat wins were posted
by Shepherd, Leep, Folse and
Richert.

Highlights at Plymouth
By Joe Stankowski
PLYMOUTH, WIS., Aug. 6
The 40 lap feature event, strict-
ly stock automobiles, held at
the fair grounds in Plymouth,
was won by Bill Mueller of
Sheboygan. Second place was
taken by Ben Denum of Elkart
followed by Al Keiser of She-
boygan. Time-49.445.
The semi feature was taken
by Don Gastoch, also of She-
boygan. This event consisted of
30 laps in which a bad roll
was taken by Jim Hansen of
Cascade. As Hansen's car rolled
he was struck by another car.
He was hospitalized, requiring
17 stitches in his leg and three
in his hand. This event was run
at 45 m.p.h. flat.
The first heat was won by
Lee Kattel of Adell; second
heat, Dick Radloff of Sheboy-
gan Falls; third heat, Hal Ku-
low of Plymouth; and fourth
heat, Ear! Grasskamp of She-
boygan.
Fastest qualifier of the eve-
ning was Wayne Bohman, She-
boygan. His time was 49.223.
