Progress at Charlotte
Charlotte, N. C.Race driver
Curtis Turner has locked horns
in many- a speedway battle in
the past 12 years, but none has
been quite so exhausting or as
rewarding as the one he entered
almost a year ago. It is just now
nearing the finish line.
"To accomplish what we have
in the last nine months has been
tougher, yet as fascinating as
anything I've ever attempted,"
declares the 35-year-old native
Virginian.
Turner was referring to the
building of the new Charlotte
Motor Speedway and the shap-
ing of plans for the first an-
nual "World 600" race for late
model sedans on June 19.
Th "We" Turner employs
means himself and the energe-
tic executive vice president and
general manager of the Speed-
way, Bruton Smith.
"To win this race against
time has required 18 to 20 hours
of our time almost every day
since we started," say Turner
and Smith.
Turner and Smith joined
forces late last spring after de-
ciding that the mounting inter-
est in stock car racing war-
ranted putting Charlotte into
the major league of this fast-
developing sport.

up with more than $400,000
with which to begin. Actual
construction began on July 19,
1959 when Lt. Gov. Luther
Barnhardt turned the first
shovel of dirt.
From that date on, it has
been a race against time and
the weather.. Three winter
snowfalls and excessive rain-
fall threatened to throw a mon-
key wrench into progress, and
almost did until Smith and
Turner moved the opening date
from May 29 to June 19.
Approximately three million
cubic yards of dirt had to be
shuffled around by giant earth-
moving equipment and Turner
and Smith ordered it done on a
day-and-night basis for several
months.
"Most construction projects
of this size would have taken
two years, maybe three," says
W. Owen Flowe, whose Char-
lotte firm has had charge of
moving the dirt. In order to
complete the job in 10 months,
Flowe has had equipment
valued at almost $3 million on
the job. He used two·11½/2-hour
shifts on numerous occasions.
All of this, general manager
Smith pointed out, is leading
up to the Speedway's grand
opening.
