WILL CHRYSLER
DO IT?
CHARLOTTE, N. C.-Will
two-time national champion
Buck Baker of Spartanburg,
S. C. drive a 1960 Chrysler in
the $106,125 "World 600" stock
car race at Charlotte Motor
Speedway on May 29?
The question hasn't been
fully answered, but Baker left
the door wide open to the possi-
bility when he filed his "World
600" entry today with Speed-
way General Manager Bruton
Smith.
Baker, who won the national
title of NASCAR's late model
division in 1956 and 1957, said
he "has been thinking seriously
about entering a Chrysler in the
World 600'." He would make
no further comment other than
_to say, "I'm considering it."
If he moves out of the Chev-
rolet he has been campaigning
this season, Baker will go to
the Chrysler New Yorker ser-
ies. The New Yorker is about
300 pounds lighter than the 300
series Baker used when he won
the national championship two
years in succession.
The possibility that Baker
will enter a Chrysler has set off
speculation among other driv-
ers as to whether a heavy car
will be a good bet in the
"World 600." Tim Flock of At-
lanta, Ga., who also once drove
a Chrysler to the national title,
says, "It might just be the
thing. I'm not saying that it
will, just that it might."
Along with Baker's entry
came that of Jim Cook of
Downey, Calif., the initial West
Coast entry in the "World 600."
Cook and his car owner, Floyd
Johnson, also of Downey, en-
tered a 1960 Dodge. Cook holds
the track record at Hanford,
Calif., and will be making his
first East Coast appearance.
"That $100,000 purse has a
lot of West Coast boys planning
to come," Cook said in tele-
phoning his entry to Speedway
headquarters at 108 · Liberty
Life Bldg., Charlotte.

Derr Wins at Hawkeye Downs
Shannon Sets
World Mark
Ernie Derr made it three
straight in 1960 winning a 150-
lap late-model feature at Cedar
Rapids, Iowa. Derr, who took
the checker with a 14.8 second
bulge over runner-up Herb
Shannon, was running well
back in the pack until late in
-
the contest when the three lead-
ing machines were forced to
the pits.
Cedar Rapids' fans, 7,250
strong and less than overjoyed
with the temporary seating
available at Hawkeye Downs,
soon forgot their inconvenience
as the "Doc Hunter groomed"
half-mile oval proved even
faster than usual. Top quali-
fier Darrell Dake turned a lap
of 26.90 (67.56 m.p.h.) in his
1960 Chevrolet, only .42 seconds
off the world's mark. The
IMCA standard for 15-laps was
broken twice during the after-
-
noon.
In the opening 7½/2-mile heat
Dake swept from last spot in
an inverted start to take the
checker in the world. record
time of 7:01.89. The dust had
hardly settled when Herb
Shannon went out and bettered
Dake's mark. Herb was driving
a 1957 Pontiac. Shannon's time,
7:01.41 (64.10 m.p.h.), knocked
a total of 11.24 seconds off the
existing record held by Johnnie
Beauchamp.
Derr, who was making his
first appearance in a 1960 Pon-
tiac, .came as close to seeing
lightning strike twice as he ever
hopes to. In the spring of 1959
Derr sold a '57 Pontiac to rookie
Dick Hutcherson only to have
Hutch give him a trimming
with the same car in the Pelican
300. This year at Cedar Rapids
another rookie, Eldon Sheffler,
was driving Derr's champion-
ship '57 Pontiac and history
almost repeated itself. Sheffler
ran a consistant second and
took the lead when Hutcherson
hashed his engine at lap 118. It
looked as though Sheffler had
a clear field to victory, but, as
