Pabst
By Pierre Perrin
Augie Pabst of Milwaukee,
in the powerful "Meister Brau-
ser" Scarab took top honors at
Elkhart Lake's beautiful Road
America sports car course Sun-
day. In the process, Pabst also
broke the fast lap record sev-
eral times, with the final figure
being an astounding 2:44:90 or
87.325 MPH over the tricky four
mile course. His main competi-
tion came from 39 year old Dr.
Dick Thompson, the flying den-
tist from Washington, D.C. in
the "Sting Ray", a highly mod-
ified, Chevrolet inspired car
owned by Bill Mitchell from
Detroit.
The first race on Saturday
featured the Corvettes in class
B Production and also C and
D production. Bob Spooner of
St. Louis took an early lead,
but soon was overtaken by
George Reed from Chicago in
a new Ferrari Berlinetta coupe.
Spooner, Reed and Jack Knap
from Dayton dueled for the re-
mainder of the race and at the
finish a blanket could cover the
two Corvettes of Spooner and
Knab, who finished in that or-
der behind Reed.
Race two on Saturday fea-
tured the fast little Formula
Juniors, which are actually scal-
ed down versions of the Euro-
pean Grand Prix machines.
Steve Wendt of Denver took
the lead after 7 laps in his Elva-
DKW and led the rest of the
way. Augie Pabst had entered
his fast Moretti, a rear engined
Fiat powered creation, but a
fractured half shaft and valve
difficulties kept the car on the
sidelines.
The first race on Sunday was
an all E Production event with
Duncan Black of Parkton,
Maryland in the lead all the
way with a Daimer. Jim Kaser
in an Austin Healey tried hard,
but never could catch Black's
