Wednesday, June 22, 1960
By VER-BET
It's nice to be a bridesmaid
but by being a bride you're the
star of the show. Bill Johnson
was just that in his powerful
modified stock car in winning
the feature event, before a chill
cloaked crowd at Slinger, Wis-
consin. Following a faulty start,
the 16 fastest stocks took off
and after the lead changed
hands seven times Johnson
stuck his radiator cap in the
front of the pack on the 24th
lap and with the aid of caution
stayed there until the end. Rid-
ing close behind as though tied
by a chain was the establisher
of a new time trial track rec-
ord (15.31—58.82 mph), the pro-
verbial Mouse, Miles Melius.
The balance of the leaders, Bill
Schwister 3rd, Etchie Biertzer
4th, and Fuzzy Fassbender 5th,
were slowed up due to Norbie
Klemp and Al Feller locking
horns on the south corner, ob-
structing traffic and causing no
end of fancy maneuvering for
the remaining drivers.
The 15 lap semi-feature had
18 cars starting with only 10
finishing. . Collisions appeared
certain throughout the evening
and the semi was no exception.
When the checkered flag
emerged through the turmoil,
Ed Hume, Portage, was first,
Doug Bandl 2nd, and Ron En-
derle 3rd.
Every one of the four 10-lap
modified stock car heat races
had its share of mishaps. The
crowd let out a howl during the
third heat when Melius went 4th.

Jim Jeffords next Saturday and
Sunday will drive in the 24 hour
endurance race at Le Mans,
France. Pabst, in 1959, won the
United States Auto Club's pro-
fessional road racing title. Jef-

"Windybagoland"
It'll be drivers, boats and mo-
tors versus 88 miles of pounding
water in the 12th annual Mil-
waukee Sentinel - Winnebago-
land Outboard Marathon, Sun-
day, June 26 at Fond du Lac,
Wisconsin.
Always a super test of the
stamina of men and their equip-
ment, the "Windybagoland" is
America's greatest and most
colorful outboard racing event.
When the starting gun sounds,
about 200 drivers will head
their stock utility runabouts
northwest from Lakeside Park,
Fond du Lac, and speed up Lake
Winnebago, go through Oshkosh
and continue on across lakes
Butte des Morts, Winneconne
and Poygan.
From there, the speedy craft
will go up the Wolf River to
Fremont, the turnaround spot.
Drivers return over the same
44 mile route to complete the
88 mile grind.
For the past 11 years, the
Sentinel-Winnebagoland Mara-
thon has been No. 1 in both
driver participation and specta-
-
tor appeal.
Drivers like the challenge of-
fered by the race. Statistics
show that over the years, it's
become an honor not only to
return a winner, but to simply
finish the race.

water is conducive to choppy
waves stirred up by just about
any breeze. In 1956, one of the
roughest years, 223 started the
gruelling run and only 21 fin-

heat, made the correct changes
and pushed his Thunderbird
powered Ford into the lead on
the 22nd lap of the special 35-
lap feature (50.91), holding the
lead to the very end. Pressing
hard behind him was Carl Jen-
sen, Mukwonago, who was mak-
ing his remarkable debut at
Wilmot, followed closely by

3rd; Norm Nelson, Racine, in his

The 35-lap feature was heart
breaking to a score of the favo-
rite drivers. Milt Curcio, Ra-
cine, broke a gear box; Jack
Frost, Zion, Ill., broke a drive
shaft; Bob Karry Waukegan,
Ill., injured his steering upon
sliding into the fences; Paul
Hollister Northbrooke, Ill., went
through the fences, and Gary
Sheldon, Beloit, lost his left
wheel. Bob Ratajczyk, Kenosha,
experienced engine malfunction
and was forced to retire to the
pit area.
The special 20-lap semi-fea-
ture established a new track
record of 49.49 mph and was
won by Rod "Herr" Fuller of
Libertyville, Ill. Twenty-two
cars started this event with only
eleven cars receiving the tri-
umphal checkered flag, trials
and tribulations claiming the
eleven cars who failed to finish.
A toothpick manufacturer could
have made a fortune on busted
fences as most were reduced to
splinters.
The four 10-lap modified
stock car heat races and the
one 10-lap amateur strictly
stock car events were hotly con-
tested and pulse disturbing. Ray
Clark, Lake Zurich, Ill.; Rod
Fuller, Racine; Russ Sorenson,
Racine; Bob Karry, Waukegan,
Ill., won the four heat races
respectively. Fritz Braatz,
Wheatland, Wis., won the 10-
lap amateur event in a 1950
Chevrolet.
Gary Mabus, Zion, Ill., was
removed from the evening com-
petition during the time trials
when his engine well caught
fire. Swift fire protection saved
the auto from total loss.
Bob Mace, Chicago, gave ev-
eryone a score when his new
fuel injected Chevrolet took
him through the southeast cor-
ner fence, plowed through the
infield, re-entered the track
through the northeast fence and
continued on his merry way in
the semi-feature.
