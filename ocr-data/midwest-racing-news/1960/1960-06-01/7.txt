By Al Krause
One of the country's foremost
auto racers who is as nice as he
is big is Bobby Veith, a 235
pound daredevil from Oakland,
Calif. Veith, who will drive
the famed Red No. 44 Pete
Schmidt Special in the June 5
100 mile national big car cham-
pionship race at State Fair
Park, has been driving big cars
and midgets since 1947.
Big Bobby's first auto racing
ride was a roadster, a popular
of racing on the west
form
coast right after World War II.
He did it mostly for a hobby
but got so good at it that he de-
cided- he'd make his living that
way. Bob switched over to the
midgets later that year and set
a qualifying record at the Oak-
land, Calif., speedway. He be-
came so good in the midgets
that he finally nailed down the
Bay Cities Midget Racing Asso-
ciation indoor champion in the
1956-'57 season.
Veith came to the midwest in
1953 driving the Ernie Ruiz
sprint car, but he had so little
success that he almost gave up
racing. However, in 1954 he
was given another ride in the
sprinters and soon became a
popular driver. In 1955 he tried
his hand at the national cham-
pionship big cars, finishing in
the money four times and pick-
ing up enough points for 12th
place.
In 1956 Veith won the "Rook-
ie of the Year" award at the
Indianapolis speedway and the
Jerry Hoyt Memorial trophy as
the most improved sprint car
driver. Veith hit the champion-
ship trail and came up with
enough points for seventh place
in the standings. That year he
turned over in a race at Atlan-
