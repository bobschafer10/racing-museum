Letters to the Editor
Gentlemen:
Last night at the Cedarburg Speedway there was a new car,
No. 83, that was new to me. The car looked like it really had.
it on the straight-away, but was sour on the turns. Could you
please tell us a little about the car-what's under the hood,
name of driver, and what other track he races, etc.
Thank you.
A Racing Fan from Port Washington:
The driver of car number 83 is Robert P. Mace (dig those
crazy initials, RPM) who resides at 5301 North Spaulding Ave-
nue, Chicago 25, Illinois.
He plans on running the following circuit: Cedarburg on
Wednesday nights, State Fair Park on Thursday nights, Hales
Corners on Saturday nights, and Waukegan on Sunday nights.
The car is newly built, sporting a Hilborne fuel injection,
283 cubic inch Chevy engine and what is left of a 1953 Stude-
baker body. The car ran as a full bodied modified last season
and was driven by Norm Lopresti of.Chicago. It has 1930 Chev-
rolet rails and is even equipped with a model A grill. Driver
Mace built the car by hand and Cedarburg, Wednesday night,
June 8, was one of its initial runs.
Mace explains that the reason the car was "sour" on the
turns was that the throttle linkage was not properly adjusted.
Consequently, while going into the turns at 3,000 rpm he had
little to no idle and was not able to back off properly. With more
adjustment, a little more mechanical work and different tires
Mace expects to be running quite well in the near future.
Chief mechanic is Harry Clopper of Bay View, Wisconsin.
