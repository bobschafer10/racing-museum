Some of you should remem-
ber when the modified stocks
first got their start. They
weren't very fast then, but were
popular because of their slam-
bang way of getting around the
track. Well, they've come a long
way since those days. Sure,
there is still some slam-banging,
me is their tremendous power
The speeds attained Saturday
night, June 4, at the Hales Cor-
ners track made me think along
these lines. The track was ex-
ceptionally fast that evening
and the one lap record was
broken three times during time
trials, the final record being set
by Billy Johnson at 17.55 sec-
onds or 59.94 mph. Granted
sixty mph does not sound very
impressive in terms of speed
on the straight and level, but
it's "flying low" on the short
dirt track ovals.
The night this record was set
the track was smooth and
"tacky," allowing the tires a
better "bite." A brief conversa-
tion with Greg Krieger, the
first of the three drivers to
break the record that evening,
enlightened me to the fact that
the highest speeds are attained
down the back stretch, the rea-
son being they can drive the east
turn faster than the west, and
consequently can "stand on it
longer." This then brings me
to another point in mind. If the
cars are averaging nearly 60
mph, how fast, then, are they
going in the straight-aways and
being more specific, how fast
are they going down the back
"chute" when the engines are
winding out at six to seven
thousand rpm.
.My interest in this speed, and
I assure you I'm not alone in
this, has led me to propose that
a speed trap be set up near the
end of the back stretch where
the machines are at their peak
in horsepower. I'm sure you,
the reader, would be interested
in knowing how fast your fav-
orite car can trip such a speed
trap. Therefore, I shall endeav-
or to push this proposal through
to the proper track officials.
