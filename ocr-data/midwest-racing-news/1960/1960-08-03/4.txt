One of the nicest guys and
toughest drivers in Milwaukee
area modified stock car racing
circles is Milt Curcio, Racine.
Curcio, who is in his 10th year
of driving, is a five foot, seven
inch, 170 pound bundle of steel
nerves and quick reflexes.
Milt, who is 37 years old this
year, drove his first race in 1939
as a member of the Racine
Motorcycle Club. The only bad
accident he's'ever had in racing
was in 1946 on a cycle. Milt
drove his first race at state fair
park, July 31, 1951. He ap-
peared in a 1940 Plymouth No.
196. His time was 24.58 seconds
in trials. The next week he
brought the car back and cut
his time trial mark to 22.35
seconds. Those two weeks he
drove he failed to finish in the
money.
Then on Aug. 7, Milt showed
up with a 1941 Mercury No.
298. His time trial was 22.98
seconds. He started in ninth
spot in the fourth eight lap heat
race. Coming out of the south
turn, Milt was tapped by an-
other car, lost control, flipped
once completely, landed on his
wheels and drove the car to the
pits for a quick inspection.
Needless to say, that ended
Milt's driving for the night.
Since that time Curcio has
come a long way. He is pres-
ently rated as one of the best
drivers in modified circles.
Earhier this year he purchased
the Mercury-powered Crosley
racer from Norm Nelson,
townsman. A short time ago
the stocky speedster won his
first main race on the fair park
oval. Earlier he had notched
main event' wins at Wilmot, in
Kenosha county.
Milt is currently running sec-
ond to Nelson in the Wilmot
poinit standings and holds down
