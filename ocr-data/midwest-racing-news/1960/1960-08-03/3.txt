Hoosier Hundred
INDIANAPOLIS, IND.-Two
more popular veterans returned
their signed entry forms to Pro-
moter Joe Quinn today, increas-
ing the official list to six for the
annual Hoosier Hundred dirt
track championship race at the
Indiana State Fairgrounds on
Saturday, Sept. 17.
Johnny Thomson of Boyer-
town, Pa., will be in the cockpit
of the Hoosier Motor Express
Special entered by Chief Me-
chanic Joe Langley of South-
port, Ind. Don Freeland of Tor-
rance, Calif., will drive the John
Zink Special entered by Chief
Mechanic Dennie Moore of
Tulsa, Okla.
It will be Thomson's fifth
Hoosier Hundred appearance.
Although eliminated twice by
mechanical trouble, he placed
second to Jimmy Bryan in 1955
and also was runner-up to Eddie
Sachs in 1958.
Freeland has driven in six of
the seven previous Hoosier
Hundred events and placed
among the first 10 finishers on
four occasions.
Both veterans also have com-
piled enviable records at the In-
dianapolis Motor Speedway,
after breaking into "big time"
competition simultaneously in
1953. Thomson finished third in
'59, fourth in '55 and fifth this
year. Freeland was third in '56
and seventh on two other occa-
sions.
The field for thé Sept. 17
event also includes Sachs, Rodg-
and Jim Hurtubise with ap-
proximately 20 additional nom-
inations expected. Time trials
will get under way at 12:30 p.m.
and the 18 fastest qualifiers will
be eligible for the feature event
at 3 p.m.
Tickets are priced at $6, $5
and $4, including federal tax
and general admission. Mail or-
ders should be addressed to the
Hoosier Hundred box office,
Indiana State Fairgrounds, In-
dianapolis 5, Ind.
Graham Killed
At Bonneville
Athol Graham of Salt Lake
City, Utah was killed, Monday
August 1, when his 3,000 horse-
power car went out of control
and flipped end over end for
some 4,000 feet before it
stopped. At the time of the ac-
ciden it was estimated he was
traveling at a speed of 300 mph.
The present world record is
394.19 mph, established by John
Cobb in 1947.
