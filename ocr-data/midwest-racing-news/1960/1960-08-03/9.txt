Bettenhausen Enters
Hoosier Hundred
INDIANAPOLIS, IND.
Tony Bettenhausen, the undis-
puted dean of all drivers still
active in championship competi-
tion on the nation's one-mile
dirt tracks, today became the
fourth entrant in the eighth
annual Hoosier Hundred at the
Indiana State Fairgrounds on
Sept. 17.
The veteran from Tinley Park,
Ill., who devotes most of his
time and energy to his own
farm when not racing, will be
at the wheel of Lindsey Hop-
'kin's year-old Dowgard special.
Official entries for the Sept.
17 event also have been received
from two former Hoosier Hun-
dred winners Roger Ward and
Eddie Sachs-and from Jim
Hurtubise, who won "Rookie of
the Year" honors at the Indian-
apolis Motor Speedway in May
after setting a new 10-mile rec-
ord of 149.056 miles an hour.
Bettenhausen, who started
his racing career in 1938 and
captured his first 100-mile dirt
track championship event in
1946, now has 22 such victories
to his credit. Although never
successful in leading the field
home at the Indiana Fair-
grounds, he has participated in
six of the seven previous Hoos-
ier Hundreds and finished in
the top 10 three times.
He won the National Driving
Championship first in 1951 and
again in 1958. He also has
placed among the top 10 at the
Indianapolis Motor Speedway
five times, finishing second in
1955 for his best performance.
His record so far this season
includes a second at Trenton in
April and a fourth at Milwau-
kee in June.
Ticket orders for the Hoosier
Hundred should be addressed to
the Box Office at the Indiana
State Fairgrounds, Indianapolis
5, Indiana. Box seats are priced
t $6, grandstand seats at $5 and
reserved bleacher seats at $4.
