AMERICA
By Pierre Perrin
Two drivers who were never
considered when the "experts"
did their forecasting for the
Road America 500 proved that
the sometimes fragile Birdcage
Maserati can win a long dis-
tance race. Dave Causey, 30, of
Carmel, Ind., and Luke Stear,
38, of Zionsville, ind. drove a
cool, well calculated race and
came back with the top silver-
ware in the grueling 500 mile
sports car race at Road Amer-
ica.
The race was led for the first
46 laps by Jim Jeffords of Mil-
waukee, the winner of the
USAC pro race at Road Amer-
ica in July, and Jim Hall of
Dallas, Tex. Their Maserati,
identcial to the winning car,
set a torrid pace until the ex-
haust manifold broke and the
cockpit became unbearably not.
In addition to the intense heat
near the accelerator pedal,
their brakes went out when the
fluid boiled out of the dual
master cylinders.
Bob Holbert in a new Porsche
RS-60 took the lead when Hall
and Jeffords ran into trouble
and for a while it looked like
the little bomb from Stuttgart
just might be a winner. How-
ever, at 2:23 (the race started
at 10:30 in the morning) Roger
Penske brought the car in with
a malfunctioning clutch and
for 22 minutes the crew worked
like beavers while their lead
slipped away. Bill Wuesthoff
of Milwaukee in the factory
entered Ferrari took the lead
for four laps until he came in
for fuel and relief on lap 70.
Augie Pabst took over and
drove like a madman, but the
car never made the front lines
again.
Another Maserati, this one a
2 litre (12z cu. in.) driven by
the great John Fitch and Bill
Kimberly (nephew of Jim)
took the lead until lap 88 when
it came in for fuel. It was on
the 89th lap that Stear, driving
the Causey Maserati, went out
in front where he remained
until the finish at 125 laps
around the four mile race
course.
A total of 44 cars started the
race and the early favorites
were Jeffords, Dr. Dick
Thompson in the beautiful
"Stingray," Holbert-Penske or
Pabst-Wuesthoff in the Ferrari.
On the 15th lap Jeffords was
way out in front with Thomp-
son second, Pabst third, Hol-
bert fourth, Walt Hansgen in
the new "" Jaguar fifth and
Causey sixth. On the 3oth lap
the order was the same, with
the exception that Holbert had
passed Pabst for third place.
The fastest car in B modified,
the Echnida from way up in
Northern Minnesota, dropped
out at 11:47 with a broken axle
and blown clutch.
