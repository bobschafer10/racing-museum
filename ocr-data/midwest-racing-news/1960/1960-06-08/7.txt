STOTT AT OWATONNA
Dick Hutcherson, a 28 year
old IMCA sophomore, fought
off the late advances of Ramo
Stott to take the bunting in a
50-mile stock car feature at
Owatonna, Minnesota, May 29.
Hutcherson was driving the
same 1957 Pontiac which he
chauffered to runnerup honors
in the 1959 point race. He beat
Stott to the finish line by a
mere car length after leading at
one time by better than 8½2
seconds.
Time trials were cancelled in
favor of a drawing as the
weather threatened. A steady
drizzle fell through the two
opening heats, but relented at
the start of the feature. The sun
burst through at the five-mile
mark and by the half-way point
the track was dry and fast. Auto
Racing, Inc. supervised.

Ford was Mert Williams ('58
Ford) and Bob Kosiske ('59 T-

were run under the yellow.

He then decided to put the
engine in a dragster. The sixth
time out, he came within 1/2-
mph of the world's record for
gas and became one of the very
few to break 10-seconds with
gas. After three months reserve
duty with Uncle Sam, Tom
broke the world's gas ET time
at 9.5. This was later lost and
regained at 9.16. This single
engine car still holds
s the
world's ET record for B class
dragsters. Ivo and the single
engine supercharged Buick also
hold the world's B-gas speed
record at 159 mph.
Since he had squeezed about
all power possible out of the
single engine, his only answer
was to go to two engines. He
wanted to stay with Buicks be-
cause through the years he
found them very dependable.
Using some ideas from his old
rail, correcting some mistakes
and coming up with some new
angles, Ivo built a new car with
the thought in mind of being
the first to break 9-seconds on
gas. After six weeks labor and
$4,600, the new twin engine
Buick dragster was ready to
roll. Many things had to be
concurred, but most important
was the weight. If the car was
too heavy, the rear tires would
break loose instead of moving
the car forward rapidly. Many
of the normal things were done
such as using cycle wheels up
front and thin wall chrome mo-
ly tubing and aluminum where
ever possible.
The unusual innovations are
the most interesting. Instead of
using two differentials, two
clutches and two drive lines,
Ivo transferred power from one
engine to the other by attach-
ing two starter ring gears to
each flywheel and tilting the
engines together side-by-side
and, then, sending all power
through a common drive line.
In order to do this, one engine
had to be made to rotate in the
opposite direction. This was
done by a special Iskenderian
made camshaft.
The differential had to be
moved to one side by shorten-
ing one rear axle. The overall
installation helped to keep the
car narrow. The engine pans
were highly modified to handle
the oiling problems of the titled
engines.
A parachute was installed on
the back of the car to help stop
it. The same type chutes are
used on jet planes to stop them
on short strips.
After solving all these prob-
lems, Ivo ran the car for the
first time and the tires smoked
all the way through the time
traps. As less throttle was used
at the start, times got better.
On the seventh trip the car ac-
complished what it was built
for, setting a new world's ET
record of 8.95 seconds on gaso-
line; the first one ever to break
the 9-second barrier on gas. La-
ter, Ivo and the Buick esta-
blished the world's 1320-feet
A-gas dragster speed record of
172.74 mph.

Folse Masters
Belleville Banks
Repeating his Memorial Day
performance on the high-
banked, half-mile oval at Belle-
ville, Kansas, Pete Folse, de-
fending sprint car champion,
took a 20-lap main event, May
30, before a standing room only
crowd of 5,500. National Speed-
ways supervised.
A field loaded with top chauf-
fers and hot equipment gave
Folse and the Honore Bardahl
Special some close attention,
but the smiling Frenchman held
off all comers to set best qual-
ifying time (21.30) and win the
opening heat, match race and
ten-mile fiñale. Pete's average
speed for the feature distance
was 74.962 mph.
Back in the familiar Chet
Wilson Chevrolet was Harold
Leep who glued himself to
Folse's nerfing bar for a second
place finish.
The day's only accident in-
volved young Dale Reed of
Wichita who slipped in the sec-
ond heat, nailed the back stretch
fence and flipped. Rescue crews
were forced to pry Reed from
his machine but a good shaking
up was the extent of his in-
juries.
