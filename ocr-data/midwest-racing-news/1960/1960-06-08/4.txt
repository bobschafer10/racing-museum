MIDWEST RACING NEWS
Badger Karting
The Badger Karting Club,
Inc., of Milwaukee, is sponsor-
ing a two day event at Elkhart
Lake's beautiful "Karting Ket-
tle," a natural amphitheatre
right in the downtown heart of
Elkhart Lake.
The event is to be run on Sat-
urday and Sunday, June 11 and
12 with technical inspection be-
ginning on both days at 8 a.m.
with races starting at 1 p.m.
A field of some 175 to 225 karts
are expected to participate.
The kart races will be staged
using all classes of the karts
and will include Class AC, stock
Clinton engines; Class A, en-
gines up to 5.8 cubic inch dis-
placement; Class A Super, same
as previous only with engines
costing over $100; Class B, en-
gines with cubic inch displace-
ment of 11.6; Class B Super,
same only with engines ranging
in cost over $200; Class C, en-
gine displacement of 16.4 cubic
inches, and Class C Super, the
same except engine value over
$300 and finally a Class X
which will include anything
else, not already mentioned.
The competing course is an
asphalted quarter-mile course
with a slight pitch to it. Each
day will include trophy compe-
tition and will consist of heat
races contested at eight laps in
each class and followed by a 15
lap semi final and a 15 lap fea-
ture event made up of the top
finishers in each previous class.
Karts from all parts of the state
and even northern Illinois and
Upper Michigan are expected
to compete. Admission is only
$1.00 and anyone, regardless of
age, who is accompanied by his/
her parents will be admitted
free of charge.

Hales Corners'
Shortened by
Johnson Sets Qualifying Mark
Falling victim to adverse weather conditions for two con-
secutive weeks, the modified stock car go at the Hales Corners
Speedway was short-lived last Saturday night when heavy
rains hit the track during the second lap of the semi-final, can-
celling the remainder of the program. A week earlier a heavy
fog had settled over the track during the feature, making the
race all but exciting to watch.
Saturday's preliminary events,
however, were jam-packed with
racing thrills. During qualify-
ing runs the one-lap record of
59.73 mph set a week ago by
Etchie Biertzer of Barton was
broken three .times. Biertzer
himself broke it once, as did
Greg Krieger of Milwaukee.
Taking the final honor was Bil-
ly Johnson of Oconomowoc, who
averaged 59.94 mph to set the
new mark.
Schwister Sets 10-Lap Mark
Billy Schwister, king of the
"three-wheeled drivers," so far
this season knocked a remark-
able five seconds off the old
track record of 56.12 mph in
the third 10-lap heat of the
evening. Ken Tlougan of Mil-
waukee had held the former
record at 56.12 mph until
Schwister's new mark of 57.5
mph was officially placed on
the books.
