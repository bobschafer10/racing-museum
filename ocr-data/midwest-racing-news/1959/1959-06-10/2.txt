In Memoriam
Herbert Johannsen, better known as "Pops" to
hundreds of race car drivers and fans, was a be-
loved man. 53 years of age, "Pops" still devoted
his dreams to the sport of racing. He had been
connected with the sport for over 25 years and lov-
ed every minute of it. He himself was a former
Midget race driver who performed in various parts
of the country under the name of "Red" Johnson.
He raced at Soldier's Field in Chicago, on the half
mile track in Cedarburg, and at the State Fair
Park in Milwaukee on both the small track and the
Coliseum, when Midget racing in that building was
popular back in the early 1930's. When Stock Car
Racing took over from the Midgets, his interests
In 1954 he bought his first Stock car for his son,
Jim, then 28 years old. Together they formed one
of the first Father-Son Racing Teams in local
Stock Car Racing. The team carried on for three
years with "Pops" as the Chief Mechanic and Jim
as the Main Driver. Their very first race was at
Hales Corners, and from there they branched out
and ran at all tracks; State Fair Park, Slinger,
Cedarburg, and others. In 1956 "Pops" decided he
would buy his own welding equipment and bring it
out to the tracks. In the pits this equipment kept
many cars running during the course of events. It
was here that "Pops" became well known among
stock car drivers and owners. He charged only a
nominal fee, enough to keep the welder running.
In all these years, he seldom, if ever, failed to bring
his equipment to the tracks. Each racing night he
could be found at one of the tracks working hard
at a job he loved; that of helping his racing friends.
It wasn't only welding, but wherever he could lend
a helping hand, a bit of advice, or an optimistic
look into the future, "Pops" was the person the driv-
ers could count upon. In 1957 Jim bought his own
car, and "Pops" engaged Nickie Dickman to drive
for him. Dickman did a good job of chauffeuring
his car, but it wasn't quite fast enough. Dickman
did manage to win several heat events and the semi-
feature event on several occasions, but was unable
to capture a feature race., In that year they raced
all the tracks on the circuit, which included Slinger
on Sunday nights, Waukegan on Tuesday nights, Ce-
darburg on Wednesday nights, State Fair Park on
Thursday. nights, and Hales Corners on Saturday
nights. In 1958 Dickman wanted to build and drive
his own car, and Dan Bantin, another good friend
of "Pops"' took over the driving. Again "Pops'"
car wasn't fast enough to make feature races and
"Pops' still dreamed about the day he would be the

standings, five points behind second place. At Ce-

seventh. But at Waukegan, where Bobby was run-
ning well in all his races, he took the checkered flag

Pops" was extremely happy and

feature car. He had a fast car and a good driver.
"Pops" Johannsen's joy was reflected in the eyes of
many drivérs and car owners who knew him so well.

Wednesday, June 10, 1959
Etchie Biertzer, Barton, is
shown taking around the check-
ered flag after winning a spec-
ial 10 lap benefit race staged
in the memory of Herbert
"Pops" Johannsen, at the Hales
Corners Speedway, Saturday
night, June 6th. Eight of the
fastest qualifiers staged the
non-money event in honor
"Pops". Eddie Stillman, Don
Schuppel, & Miles Melius gave
Biertzer some real hot compe-
tition as the three finished two,
three, & four, respectively in a
very close race all the way.
Fans, drivers and pit men, con-
tributed to a special memorial
fund for "Pops" at Hales Cor-
ners Saturday & Waukegan
Sunday. A total of $421.00 was
collected at the two tracks and
turned over to "Pops'" wife,
Mrs. Lillian Johannsen. Anoth-
er collection is scheduled for
Cedarburg, this week.
