ed in velocity but all divisions
managed a perfect start. The
last start, that of the A's and
B's, became handicapped as
light rain started falling and
lightning was noticeable to the
west. However, the threatening
skys did not seem to bother
drivers, as they later revealed
the only thought on their
minds was the race ahead.
One of the roughest parts of
the race developed at Lake
Butte des Morts as the wind
swept across this area.

Miller, led the major part of
his race with a few exceptions,

utes and 34 seconds slower than

Class C winner was Mike
Harris, a 16 year old machine
shop worker, from Fort
Wayne, Indiana. Harris trailed
throughout the race taking
over the lead only with a half
mile to go. Second place went
to Bob Smith of Dayton, Ohio
who relinquished the lead to
Harris because, as he put it,
"I just couldn't take the rough
water." Smith, like many or
the drivers, was numb and stiff
at the end of the race. Many
drivers had to be massaged, at
the completion of the race, be-
fore they could walk. However,
all drivers recovered quickly.
