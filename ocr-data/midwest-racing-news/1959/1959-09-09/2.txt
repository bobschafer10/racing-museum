SPEED-O-RAMA SWEEPINGS
By Al Krause
Sweeping all three races dur-
ing state fair week not only
gave Rodger Ward a new rec-
ord for vietories. It also pour-
ed into the Ward coffers $12,-
033 in prize money, a nice
week's work in any business.
Ward split will be considerable
even after he and Bob Wilke,
car owner, divvy up. Ward's
prize money take would have
been even greater if the 200
mile stock car race, Thursday,
Aug. 27 had not been rained
out.
The three races during fair
week on the fair park one mile
paved track attracted 44,913
fans and paid prize money of
$50,040. The prize money in-
cudes lap money for the 200
mile big car race as well as
money put up by Firestone
Tire Co., Champion Spark Plug
Co., Goodyear Tire Co., Mobil
Oil Co., and Perfeet Circle Pis-
ton Ring Co. To each of these
firms, race director Tom Mar-
chese sends his thanks for par-
ticipation in this year's pro-
grams, as well as those firms
who contributed lap prize
money.
If a hard luck trophy were to
be given out, it would have to
go to Tony Bettenhausen, the
people's choice here at the fair
park. In Sunday's 150 mile
stock car race, the 1958 Mer-
cury Tony drove didn't handle
as he would like it. He then
came back to lead both the 100
mile midget and 200 mile big
car races. A 30 cent fuel line
fitting broke on the Howard
Linne Offy in the midget grind
while Tony was firmly in com-
mand. An oil leak in the cock-
pit of the John Zink big car
squirted oil in his goggles and
when Tony tried to wipe off the
oil, the rag flew across his face,
