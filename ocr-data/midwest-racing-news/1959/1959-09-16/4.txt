TO MILES MELIUS
SLINGER ACE SETS 75-LAP TRACK MARK
Cedarburg, Sept. 9 - Al-
though it wasn't an easy win,
Miles Melius, Slinger, scored
his sixth Cedarburg victory in
the 75-lap headline event Wed-
nesday night, September 9. In
capturing, the race, Melius set
the 75-lap track record with an
average speed of 47.29 mph.
Fuzzy Fassbender, Slinger,
took the lead on the sixth lap
and drove at a terrific pace
until Melius caught him on the
64th. Melius had moved into
third position from Carl Kulow,
Plymouth, on the 34th. Billy
Schwister, Milwaukee, who had
been running second from the
20th lap, dropped to third
position on the 6lst when Me-
lius took over.
From the 45th through the
60th laps, the battle for first
place had a crowd of some 2400
persons cheering wildly as
Fassbender, Schwister and Me-
lius drove at a bumper-to-
bumper pace. At times, the
three were running in a dead
tie for first spot on the
straightaways, falling back in-
to the three positions, as they.
slowed down for the turns.
When Melius finally did take
the lead, the battle still was
not over as Fassbender and
Schwister regained the position
from Fassbender on the 69th.
Carl Kulow was still in the
running, and on thé 72nd took
3rd away from Fassbender and
the four cars finished the 75-
laps less than a car's length
behindone another.
Don Schuppel, Kewaskum,
placed fifth, while Etchie Biert-
zer, Barton, took sixth, Billy
Johnson, Oconomowoc, seventh
and Norbie Klemp , Fredonia,
eighth.
Knapton's Car Burns
After the completion of the
75-lap, Russ Knapton of Mil-
waukee, who had placed elev-
enth, had no sooner pulled into
the pit area and parked his
car, when a short circuit in his
car's electrical system ignited,
setting his car ablaze. Serious
damage resulted; but no injur-
ies occurred as the fire was
extinguished by track officials.
Fran Seamars, Milwaukee,
took the lead on the first lap
and went on to win the 15-lap
semi-feature with an average
speed of 45.64 mph. Lloyd
Keller, Kewaskum, placed sec-
ond while Buzz Gleason of Mil-
waukee took third.
In the first heat event of the
evening, Paul Coen, Milwaukee,
took the lead on the third lap
and went on to place first
ahead of John Garner, Muske-
go, and John Kraemer of West
Bend. Royal Zetting, Milwau-
kee, caused a three car accident
in this event when he spun on
the north, turn and was hit by
John Reiser and Tom Suhm.
Seamars captured the second
heat of the evening when he
took the lead from Lloyd Kel-
ler when Keller spun out on the
fourth lap. Homer Ansell and
Bill Goeden, who were running
in second and third spots, col-
lided, spinning them both out,
and Harvey Wirth took over
second. Keller had moved back
through the field to place third
at the finish.
