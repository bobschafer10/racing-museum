Your Car Should Be
"Posturized"
When you buy a new car,
you "try it for size?"
That question isn't as absurd
as it may sound. Position and
rigidity of. the seats, location of
operating devices and accessor-
ies, height of the windows, etc.,
all contribute to the development
of desirable or negative posture
habits.
When selecting the family au-
tomobile, the comfort of parents
and children alike should be con-
sidered. Strains caused by shump-
ing or craning can influence dis-
position and attitudes, can turn
what might otherwise be a pleas-
ant trip into a bickering contest.
What's more, fatigue induced by
poor posture could lead to a ser-
ious accident.
Here are some of the features
that call for special attention.
Windshields and windows should
provide a clear view for both
driver and passengers - without
taxing the nerves and blood ves-
sels. Steering wheels should be
positioned in such a manner that
crouching is unnecessary.
Look for seats that are easily
adjusted, that assure ample leg
room for everyone. For best sup-
port, cushions will be firm, but
resilient enough to absorb the
severest shock.
Be sure that dash board in-
struments and accessories are
within easy reach, thus elimina-
ting muscle-straining groping.
The car doors should permit easy
entry and exit, so that passen-
gers don't have to contort their
bodies while getting in or out.
Follow this check list, advises
your doctor of chiropractic, and
your reward will be countless
hours of happy motoring.
